## Executive summary

For the people who defend networks, 2024 was a year of two doors and one long shadow. The first door was the appliance at the edge: the year opened with Ivanti Connect Secure gateways exploited as zero-days, an emergency directive that told federal agencies to apply Ivanti's updates within 48 hours of release, and a joint advisory that found the vendor's own integrity checker had missed compromise; Palo Alto Networks, Cisco, Fortinet, ConnectWise, Check Point and Cleo products followed. The second door was a password with no second factor behind it: a legacy test account at Microsoft, customer accounts at a cloud data platform, and VPN logins for ransomware affiliates. The long shadow was the state actor that waits: US and allied agencies said a PRC state-sponsored group had held footholds in some US critical infrastructure networks for at least five years, and by November the FBI and CISA were describing a broad espionage campaign inside multiple telecommunications companies. Against that, law enforcement struck ransomware hard. Operation Cronos took control of LockBit's administration environment, its leader was named and sanctioned, coordinated operations hit droppers and infostealers, and Treasury sanctioned the operators of a residential proxy botnet. The outage that crashed an estimated 8.5 million Windows devices on 19 July was no attack at all, but a faulty security update. Governments answered with emergency directives, sanctions on Chinese, Russian, Iranian and criminal actors, a ban on one antivirus vendor's sales, CSF 2.0, the first post-quantum standards, a UN cybercrime convention and the NIS2 deadline.

This study is the dated record of that year. It is a chronicle, not a deep dive: where another study in this series is the home for an event, this one gives a paragraph and a link. Change Healthcare is told in [the Change Healthcare study](/case-studies/CHANGE_HEALTHCARE_2024.md); the XZ Utils backdoor in [the XZ Utils study](/case-studies/XZ_UTILS_BACKDOOR_2024.md); FrostyGoop, Fuxnet and the year's other industrial events in [the ICS attacks and advisories study](/case-studies/ICS_ATTACKS_AND_ADVISORIES_2024_2026.md); the longer records of the state actors in [the China-attributed](/case-studies/THREAT_ACTORS_CHINA.md), [Russia-attributed](/case-studies/THREAT_ACTORS_RUSSIA.md), [Iran-attributed](/case-studies/THREAT_ACTORS_IRAN.md) and [North Korea-attributed](/case-studies/THREAT_ACTORS_NORTH_KOREA.md) actor studies and in [the Sandworm study](/case-studies/SANDWORM_OPERATIONS.md); and the commercial spyware market in [the zero-day market study](/case-studies/ZERO_DAY_MARKET_EVENTS.md). The OpenSSH regreSSHion flaw belongs to [the SSHole OpenSSH study](/case-studies/SSHOLE_CVE_2026_123158.md), which places it in the lineage of OpenSSH flaws. Everything else in 2024 that the verified record supports is here, in date order, with the source behind each line.

Evidence grade: the events, attributions, sanctions, rules and laws are **Confirmed** from the government agency, the victim's own statement or SEC filing, the vendor that observed the activity, the CVE, NVD and KEV records, or the MITRE record. Annual estimates from vendor datasets (Chainalysis payments, Coveware's payment rate, Lumen's botnet counts) and statements a vendor relays from others are **Reported** and attributed by name. Checks this study re-ran against the library's ATT&CK, CWE, CAPEC and D3FEND data are **Demonstrated**. Where this study draws its own conclusion or maps behavior to ATT&CK on its own reading, it says **Inferred**.

### Key findings

- **The edge kept falling, and the edge lied about itself.** Ivanti's internal and external Integrity Checker Tool did not detect compromise in CISA's incident responses, and CISA lab research showed root-level persistence could survive a factory reset (Confirmed [5]). Palo Alto Networks, Cisco ASA and FortiManager appliances were exploited before or as their fixes shipped (Confirmed [32][33][91][69]).
- **A missing second factor opened the year's most cited intrusions.** The Microsoft test account Midnight Blizzard sprayed had no MFA; the Snowflake customer accounts UNC5537 used had no MFA and some credentials had not been rotated for up to four years; Akira affiliates came in through VPNs without MFA (Confirmed [132][42][36]; Inferred for the pattern).
- **Infostealers fed the cloud breaches.** Mandiant says at least 79.7 percent of the accounts UNC5537 used had prior credential exposure, the earliest linked infostealer infection dates to November 2020, and contractor devices used for personal activity were a frequent source (Confirmed [42]).
- **The PRC was inside critical infrastructure for the long term.** AA24-038A assesses with high confidence that Volt Typhoon was pre-positioning on IT networks to move to OT and disrupt Communications, Energy, Transportation and Water functions, including in Guam (Confirmed [13]). The FBI and CISA described stolen call records, compromised communications of a limited number of people in government or politics, and copied information subject to US law enforcement requests under court orders at telecommunications companies (Confirmed [75]).
- **Ransomware was hit hard, and payments fell.** Cronos took LockBit's administration environment and leak site and obtained over 1,000 decryption keys; the NCA says more than 7,000 attacks were built with LockBit's services between June 2022 and February 2024 (Confirmed [93][16][18]). Chainalysis estimates payments fell 35% (Reported [99]).
- **Crime and state blurred.** AA24-241A says Iran-based actors sold network access to ransomware affiliates for a share of the payment; AA24-207A says North Korea's Andariel funded espionage with ransomware against US healthcare (Confirmed [59][55]).
- **The worst outage was self-inflicted.** CrowdStrike's faulty Channel File 291 passed its Content Validator because of a bug; CISA and CrowdStrike both say it was not malicious (Confirmed [138][51][50]).
- **Governments used every lever.** Two emergency directives, sanctions on IRGC officers, APT31-linked nationals, LockBit's leader, Evil Corp, Sichuan Silence and the Intellexa consortium, a Commerce ban on Kaspersky, and new binding rules in the UK and the EU (Confirmed [4][10][12][28][19][65][79][26][121][128][129]).

### Top recommendations

1. **Put phishing-resistant MFA on every remote and cloud entry point, test and legacy accounts included.** The three 2024 #StopRansomware advisories on Black Basta, Akira and RansomHub put it second only to patching known exploited vulnerabilities, and the year's three most cited credential intrusions turned on its absence (Confirmed [39][36][60][132][42]; Inferred for the reading).
2. **Patch edge appliances on the KEV clock and assume an exploited appliance is lost.** AA24-060B asks for patching within 24 to 48 hours, outbound limits on SSL VPN appliances and reset of credentials stored on them, and says the vendor's integrity tool is not enough to rule out compromise (Confirmed [5]).
3. **Hunt for infostealer exposure and rotate what it found.** Mandiant recommends universal MFA, credential rotation, network allow lists and control of contractor devices (Confirmed [42]).
4. **Govern cloud applications and service principals.** Microsoft asks defenders to audit privileges of identities, service principals and apps, review apps holding broad mailbox roles, and hunt with audit logs because IP indicators are unreliable (Confirmed [132]).
5. **Log off the device and watch configuration.** The telecom guidance asks for a central configuration store with change alerting, secure centralized logging, out-of-band management and no internet-exposed management interfaces (Confirmed [77]).
6. **Stage your own updates, including security software.** CrowdStrike's commitments after 19 July were canary and staggered rollout and customer control of update timing; apply the same rule to every agent with kernel access (Confirmed [138]; Inferred for the generalization).

## Scope and method

This study covers intrusions, exploited vulnerabilities, crime, outages and the government response in calendar year 2024. It draws on CISA, FBI, NSA and partner advisories, alerts and directives, the Cyber Safety Review Board, Treasury, the UK NCA, NCSC and government, Europol, the Federal Register, NIST, the White House archive, the European Commission, the Australian Federal Register of Legislation and UNODC, the victims' own statements and SEC filings (Microsoft, HPE, loanDepot, UnitedHealth Group, Live Nation, AT&T, Sonic Automotive, Halliburton, American Water, Krispy Kreme, Cloudflare, Dropbox, BeyondTrust, Synnovis), the vendors that observed the activity (Volexity, Mandiant, Microsoft, Cisco Talos, Palo Alto Networks and Unit 42, Fortinet, ConnectWise, Claroty, Sygnia, Google TAG, CrowdStrike, Qualys), the CVE, NVD and KEV records, the live MITRE ATT&CK record for Salt Typhoon and the library's own data files. Every source was opened on 11 November 2026. Quotations are kept to a few words. Tradecraft is described at the level the advisories use, by technique name and id; no exploit details, commands, payload strings or ransom notes are reproduced.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confirmed | A primary source was opened: the agency, the victim, the vendor that observed the activity, a CVE, NVD or KEV record, or the MITRE record itself |
| Reported | Known only through a secondary source or a vendor's estimate from its own dataset, attributed by name, such as a Chainalysis figure or a claim a vendor relays from others |
| Demonstrated | A reproduction exists: this study re-ran the check against the library's data files |
| Inferred | This study's own reasoning from the cited facts, labelled where it appears |

How the record is routed:

- **Home studies.** Where another study in this series is the home for an event, this study gives one paragraph and a link: Change Healthcare ([the Change Healthcare study](/case-studies/CHANGE_HEALTHCARE_2024.md)), XZ Utils ([the XZ Utils study](/case-studies/XZ_UTILS_BACKDOOR_2024.md)) and the industrial events ([the ICS attacks and advisories study](/case-studies/ICS_ATTACKS_AND_ADVISORIES_2024_2026.md)). OpenSSH's regreSSHion has its home in [the SSHole OpenSSH study](/case-studies/SSHOLE_CVE_2026_123158.md).
- **Attribution language.** Each source's own words are carried through, because the strength varies: "high confidence" for Volt Typhoon's pre-positioning (AA24-038A), "has reason to believe" for UTA0178 as a Chinese nation-state-level actor (Volexity), "suspected espionage" with no origin assessed for UNC5221 (Mandiant), "PRC-affiliated actors" with no group name for the telecom campaign (FBI and CISA), "almost certainly part of the SVR" for APT29 (AA24-057A) and "hallmarks of a sophisticated state-sponsored actor" with no state named for ArcaneDoor (Talos) (Confirmed [13][2][84][75][87][91]; Inferred for the comparison). A vendor's name for a cluster (UTA0178, UNC5221, UTA0218, UNC5537, UNC5820, UAT4356) is used with that vendor's report; its ATT&CK object is given only where the library's data carries the name or a campaign record ties it.
- **Victims who name no actor.** The loanDepot, Live Nation, AT&T, Sonic Automotive (CDK Global), Halliburton, American Water and Krispy Kreme filings name no actor, and the AT&T and Live Nation filings do not name Snowflake (Confirmed [11][43][44][46][57][67][80]). No ransomware group is tied here to Synnovis, CDK Global, Halliburton or Krispy Kreme, because no primary source opened for this study makes the tie.
- **Revised advisories.** AA24-109A (Akira) was updated on 13 November 2025 and its live tables print ATT&CK v18 ids and later CVEs; AA24-131A (Black Basta) was revised on 8 November 2024; AA23-353A (ALPHV Blackcat) was updated on 27 February 2024 (Confirmed [36][39][97]). Where a live page mixes later material into a 2024 advisory, this study says so and does not date the later material to 2024.
- **KEV dates and flags.** A KEV date-added value is the day CISA listed the CVE, not the day it was first exploited. The known-ransomware flag is revised after listing, so the 44 flags counted here reflect the catalog of November 2026, not what was known in 2024 (Confirmed [3]; Inferred for the caution).
- **ATT&CK versions.** The 2024 advisories print ATT&CK v14 to v18 ids. This study maps them to v20.0 through the library's `superseded_by.jsonl`, where T1562.001 becomes T1685 Disable or Modify Tools, T1562.004 becomes T1686 Disable or Modify System Firewall and T1070.001 becomes T1685.005 Clear Windows Event Logs (Confirmed [140]; Demonstrated [146]).
- **Events before 2024.** HPE's mailbox compromise (from May 2023), Midnight Blizzard's spray (from late November 2023), Cloudflare's Thanksgiving intrusion (23 November 2023), ArcaneDoor's infrastructure (from November 2023) and the Ivanti exploitation (from December 2023) began in 2023 but were disclosed or acted on in 2024, and appear on the date of the 2024 record. The Nearest Neighbor intrusion was detected in February 2022 and reported in November 2024 (Confirmed [6][7][1][91][2][88]).
- **What is overstated and what is understated.** This study's own reading of where the 2024 record misleads is set out under Cross-cutting themes, with the evidence for each point.
- **What was not checked.** The following reported events and documents were not opened at a primary source during the research and are left out of the dated record: the Ascension ransomware incident; the Transport for London incident; the Port of Seattle incident; the Internet Archive breach; PowerSchool, Rhode Island's RIBridges, Blue Yonder and the Cyberhaven browser extension; the MITRE NERVE intrusion; the AnyDesk production incident; the Treasury Departmental Offices compromise reported through BeyondTrust; National Public Data; the DOJ releases on the KV botnet disruption, the Flax Typhoon botnet disruption, the APT31 indictment, the Rim Jong Hyok indictment, the Iranian campaign-hack indictment, the LockBit developer charges, the Scattered Spider charges and the 911 S5 arrest; the joint ODNI, FBI and CISA statement on Iranian election influence; Microsoft's October 2024 Star Blizzard domain action beyond the release cited; Lumen's Versa Director report; the Huntress report on Cleo and any Cl0p attribution for Cleo; the ransomware groups behind Synnovis, Ascension, CDK Global and Port of Seattle; the ALPHV exit date; Dark Angels' victim; the EU Cyber Resilience Act and AI Act Official Journal texts; and the FCC Cyber Trust Mark. Their absence here is a gap in what was opened, not a judgment on what happened.

## Part 2: the edge-device year

**Ivanti Connect Secure.** Volexity detected web shells and lateral movement at a customer in the second week of December 2023 and traced them to two zero-days in Ivanti Connect Secure, CVE-2023-46805 and CVE-2024-21887; in its post of 10 January it tracks the actor as UTA0178 and says it "has reason to believe" UTA0178 is a Chinese nation-state-level actor (Confirmed [2]). Mandiant tracked the same exploitation, from as early as December 2023, as UNC5221, a "suspected espionage" actor, and said at publication it lacked the data to assess the actor's origin (Confirmed [84]). ATT&CK records the campaign as [C0029 Cutting Edge](/mitre/campaigns/C0029.md) (Confirmed [84]; Demonstrated [143]). NVD lists CWE-287 for CVE-2023-46805 and CWE-77 for CVE-2024-21887; both entered KEV on 10 January and both now carry the known-ransomware flag (Confirmed [144][3]). Ivanti's advisory adds CVE-2024-21888 and CVE-2024-21893, a server-side request forgery (CWE-918) in the SAML component, with patches shipping from 31 January; KEV listed CVE-2024-21893 that day (Confirmed [101][145][3]). Emergency Directive 24-01 of 19 January told federal civilian agencies to apply the mitigation, remove compromised products and apply updates within 48 hours of release (Confirmed [4][102]). AA24-060B of 29 February says actors chained all three CVEs, that Ivanti's internal and external Integrity Checker Tool failed to detect compromise in CISA's incident responses, and that CISA's own lab research showed root-level persistence could survive a factory reset; it calls the tool not sufficient to detect compromise and asks organizations to weigh the risk of continuing to run the gateways (Confirmed [5]).

**ConnectWise ScreenConnect.** ConnectWise received a report of an authentication bypass on 13 February, validated it on 14 February, mitigated its cloud instances within 48 hours and told on-premises partners to upgrade; its bulletin is dated 19 February (Confirmed [20]). **Palo Alto Networks, twice.** Volexity saw zero-day exploitation of PAN-OS GlobalProtect at a customer on 10 April, found test exploitation back to 26 March, tracks the actor as UTA0218, and saw it export device configuration, move laterally and deploy the UPSTYLE Python backdoor (Confirmed [32]). Palo Alto Networks' advisory of 12 April describes CVE-2024-3400 as command injection allowing unauthenticated root code execution, discovered in production use (Confirmed [33]). NVD lists CWE-77 and the CNA lists CWE-20 and CWE-77, CVSS 10.0; KEV listed it on its publication day with known ransomware use (Confirmed [134][3]). ATT&CK records the campaign as [C0048 Operation MidnightEclipse](/mitre/campaigns/C0048.md) and the backdoor as [S1164 UPSTYLE](/mitre/software/S1164.md) (Confirmed [32]; Demonstrated [143][92]). ## Part 3: China

The wider record is in [the China-attributed actors study](/case-studies/THREAT_ACTORS_CHINA.md), and APT41's in [the APT41 public record study](/case-studies/APT41_PUBLIC_RECORD.md). This Part gives the 2024 sequence.

**Volt Typhoon.** AA24-038A of 7 February, from CISA, NSA and FBI with DOE, EPA, TSA and the Australian, Canadian, UK and New Zealand agencies, names Volt Typhoon ([G1017](/mitre/groups/G1017.md)) as PRC state-sponsored and assesses with high confidence that it is pre-positioning on IT networks to enable lateral movement to OT assets and disrupt functions primarily in Communications, Energy, Transportation Systems and Water and Wastewater, in the continental US and in territories including Guam (Confirmed [13]). It says the actors held access and footholds in some victim IT environments for at least five years, calls living off the land a hallmark of their activity, and names FortiGate CVE-2022-42475 as a likely initial access vector (Confirmed [13]). The advisory says the actors used end-of-life Cisco and NETGEAR SOHO routers implanted with KV Botnet malware (T1584.005); ATT&CK records [C0035 KV Botnet Activity](/mitre/campaigns/C0035.md) (Confirmed [13]; Demonstrated [143]). Its technique tables cover more than fifty Enterprise techniques; the research read the page to character 100,000 of 132,303, so the appendix tables were only partly read, and Part 13 binds the ids that were read (Confirmed [13]). The top mitigations put patching internet-facing systems, KEV entries and edge appliances first, then phishing-resistant MFA, no credentials stored on edge devices, least privilege, limited RDP, centralized logging, OT segmentation and replacement of end-of-life devices (Confirmed [13]). ATT&CK also records [C0039 Versa Director Zero Day Exploitation](/mitre/campaigns/C0039.md) for Volt Typhoon in June to August 2024; its primary report could not be opened, so it is named here and not bound (Demonstrated [143]).

**APT31 and the UK Electoral Commission.** On 25 March the UK said the NCSC assesses the Electoral Commission's systems were highly likely compromised by a Chinese state-affiliated entity between late 2021 and October 2022, and that APT31 almost certainly ran reconnaissance against UK parliamentarians in a separate 2021 campaign, with no parliamentary accounts compromised; the UK summoned the Chinese Ambassador and sanctioned a front company and two APT31 members (Confirmed [27]). The Electoral Commission compromise is not attributed to APT31 by name (Confirmed [27]). Treasury designated Wuhan Xiaoruizhi Science and Technology Co. (Wuhan XRZ), which it describes as an MSS front company set up by the Hubei State Security Department in 2010, and Zhao Guangzong and Ni Gaobin as APT31-affiliated, for operations against US critical infrastructure entities; the same day DOJ unsealed indictments against them and five others and State offered a reward (Confirmed [28]). In ATT&CK, APT31 is an alias of [G0128 ZIRCONIUM](/mitre/groups/G0128.md) (Demonstrated [92]).

**APT40.** AA24-190A of 8 July, led by ASD's ACSC with CISA, NSA, FBI and partners, covers APT40, previously reported as based in Haikou and tasked by the MSS Hainan State Security Department (Confirmed [49]). It says the group rapidly adapts public proof-of-concept exploits, exploits new public vulnerabilities within hours or days, prefers public-facing infrastructure to phishing, uses web shells (T1505.003), targets end-of-life devices and uses compromised SOHO devices as last-hop redirectors (T1584.008) (Confirmed [49]). In ATT&CK, APT40 is [G0065 Leviathan](/mitre/groups/G0065.md) (Demonstrated [92]).

**Velvet Ant and UNC3886.** Sygnia reported on 3 June that the China-nexus group Velvet Ant ([G1047](/mitre/groups/G1047.md)) abused F5 load balancers for persistence during a prolonged intrusion at a large organization (Confirmed [85]). Mandiant's 18 June report describes UNC3886 ([G1048](/mitre/groups/G1048.md)) as a suspected China-nexus espionage actor it has investigated since finding ESXi hypervisor malware in September 2022 (Confirmed [86]).

**Flax Typhoon and the botnet.** An FBI, CNMF and NSA advisory of 18 September (JCSA-20240918-001) says Integrity Technology Group, a PRC-based company, ran a botnet from mid-2021 that held over 260,000 devices as of June 2024; the FBI found the activity consistent with the group known publicly as Flax Typhoon, RedJuliett and Ethereal Panda, with the caveat that vendor groupings may not map 1:1 to the US Government's (Confirmed [82]). The NCSC and partners describe the same botnet of compromised routers, firewalls and IoT devices (Confirmed [62]). Lumen's Black Lotus Labs calls the botnet Raptor Train, assesses it is likely run by Flax Typhoon, and counts over 60,000 active devices at its June 2023 peak and more than 200,000 conscripted over time (Reported [120]). The library has no Flax Typhoon group object, so it stays unlinked (Demonstrated [92]).

**The telecom campaign.** A joint FBI and CISA statement of 13 November says PRC-affiliated actors compromised multiple telecommunications companies in a broad and significant cyber espionage campaign, enabling theft of customer call records, compromise of the private communications of a limited number of people mainly in government or political activity, and copying of information subject to US law enforcement requests under court orders (Confirmed [75]). On 3 and 4 December, CISA, NSA, FBI, ASD's ACSC, CCCS and NCSC-NZ issued Enhanced Visibility and Hardening Guidance for Communications Infrastructure, which says the exploitation matched existing weaknesses with no novel activity observed as of release (Confirmed [76][77]). Neither statement uses the name Salt Typhoon (Confirmed [75][77]). ATT&CK's [G1045 Salt Typhoon](/mitre/groups/G1045.md) describes a PRC state-backed actor, active since at least 2019, that compromised US telecom and ISP network infrastructure through public-facing edge devices; binding the 2024 statements to G1045 is this study's reading from the ATT&CK record, not the CISA pages of that year (Inferred [83][92]).

**Sichuan Silence.** On 10 December Treasury sanctioned Sichuan Silence Information Technology Co. and employee Guan Tianfeng over the compromise of about 81,000 firewalls through a zero-day between 22 and 25 April 2020, which included attempted Ragnarok ransomware; DOJ unsealed an indictment and Rewards for Justice offered up to US$10 million (Confirmed [79]). The 2020 event itself is in [the 2020 chronicle](/case-studies/CHRONICLE_2020.md).

## Part 4: Russia

The wider record is in [the Russia-attributed actors study](/case-studies/THREAT_ACTORS_RUSSIA.md), and Sandworm's in [the Sandworm study](/case-studies/SANDWORM_OPERATIONS.md). APT29's earlier supply-chain operation is in [the SolarWinds study](/case-studies/SOLARWINDS_SUNBURST.md).

**Midnight Blizzard at Microsoft.** Microsoft detected a nation-state attack on its corporate systems on 12 January and attributes it to Midnight Blizzard, the Russian state-sponsored actor also known as Nobelium; from late November 2023 a password spray compromised a legacy non-production test tenant account, whose permissions the actor used to access a very small percentage of corporate email, including senior leadership, cybersecurity and legal staff, and to exfiltrate some emails (Confirmed [7][114]). Microsoft disclosed it in a Form 8-K filed on 19 January (Confirmed [114]). On 25 January Microsoft's responder guidance said the sprayed account had no MFA; the actor used residential proxies and few attempts per account, then abused a legacy test OAuth application, created OAuth applications and a user to consent to them, granted the Exchange Online full_access_as_app role and read mail through Exchange Web Services (Confirmed [132]). On 8 March Microsoft said the actor had used information from the exfiltrated email to access some source code repositories and internal systems, with no evidence that customer-facing systems were compromised (Confirmed [8]). The 8-K/A filed that day says the actor continues to try to use the information, that password-spray volume rose as much as tenfold in February, that some secrets customers had shared with Microsoft by email were exposed, and that the response requires a sustained, significant commitment of resources (Confirmed [9][115]). ATT&CK carries [S1070 Black Basta](/mitre/software/S1070.md), [S0650 QakBot](/mitre/software/S0650.md), [G1024 Akira](/mitre/groups/G1024.md), [S1129 Akira](/mitre/software/S1129.md) and [S1212 RansomHub](/mitre/software/S1212.md); Phobos has no ATT&CK object in the library (Demonstrated [92]).

## Part 12: the defender view

**Patch the edge on the KEV clock, then distrust the appliance.** AA24-060B asks defenders to limit outbound traffic from SSL VPN appliances, patch within 24 to 48 hours, reset credentials stored on the appliance and weigh the risk of keeping it in service, because the vendor's integrity tool missed compromise and persistence survived a factory reset in CISA's lab (Confirmed [5]). AA24-038A asks that edge appliances and KEV entries be patched first and that no credentials be stored on edge devices (Confirmed [13]). AA24-317A adds checking for compromise before patching (Confirmed [74]).

**MFA everywhere, including where nobody looks.** The account Midnight Blizzard sprayed was a legacy test account without MFA; the UNC5537 accounts had no MFA and stale credentials; Akira came through VPNs without MFA (Confirmed [132][42][36]). Each of the three 2024 #StopRansomware advisories on Black Basta, Akira and RansomHub puts phishing-resistant MFA second only to patching (Confirmed [39][36][60]).

**Govern the cloud's non-human identities.** Microsoft's guidance asks defenders to audit privileges of identities, service principals and applications, review apps holding EWS.AccessAsUser.All or full_access_as_app, use RBAC for applications, App governance and Entra ID Protection risk-based MFA, and hunt with audit logs because IP indicators are unreliable (Confirmed [132]).

**Treat infostealer exposure as a breach.** Mandiant recommends universal MFA, credential rotation, network allow lists and control of contractor devices (Confirmed [42]).

**Log off the device and alert on configuration change.** The telecom guidance asks for a central configuration store with change alerting, flow monitoring, secure centralized logging, no internet-exposed management, out-of-band management, disabled insecure protocols, phishing-resistant MFA for router administration and tracking of end-of-life equipment (Confirmed [77]).

**Plan for your own vendor's failure.** CrowdStrike's commitments after 19 July are canary and staggered rollout and customer control of update timing (Confirmed [138]). Backups that are offline and immutable remain the ransomware advisories' recovery control (Confirmed [60]).

**Hunting cues from the record.** At the advisory level, and without commands. Each cue is this study's reading of a behavior the sources describe (behaviors Confirmed; cues Inferred):

- Edge appliances: web shells and unexpected files on VPN gateways and firewalls, configuration exports from firewalls and management planes, and outbound connections from SSL VPN appliances (Confirmed [5][32][69]).
- Cloud identity: new OAuth applications or users that consent to them, applications granted full mailbox access roles, and mail read through Exchange Web Services by unexpected applications (Confirmed [132]).
- Credentials: sign-ins to cloud data platforms from accounts with no MFA, logins with credentials seen in infostealer logs, and sprays against service and dormant accounts (Confirmed [42][87]).
- Living off the land: domain controller credential extraction and event log clearing outside change windows, and proxying through SOHO devices (Confirmed [13]).
- Ransomware precursors: remote access tools appearing where they are not used, email bombing followed by help-desk calls or Teams messages, and attempts to disable security tools (Confirmed [39][60]).

**Exposure management.** See the library's [Vulnerability Prioritization Reference](/VULNERABILITY_PRIORITIZATION_REFERENCE.md), [Edge Device Security Reference](/EDGE_DEVICE_SECURITY_REFERENCE.md), [Identity Security Reference](/IDENTITY_SECURITY_REFERENCE.md) and [Ransomware Defense Reference](/RANSOMWARE_DEFENSE_REFERENCE.md).

## Cross-cutting themes

- **The edge was the front door again, and its self-checks failed (Inferred).** Ivanti, PAN-OS, Cisco ASA, FortiManager and ScreenConnect were exploited at or before disclosure, and AA24-060B found the vendor's integrity tool missed compromise (Confirmed [2][32][91][69][20][5]). See [Edge Device Security](/EDGE_DEVICE_SECURITY_REFERENCE.md).
- **Identity was the perimeter (Inferred).** Microsoft's corporate mail, the Snowflake customer tenants and Akira's VPN entries all turned on a credential without MFA, and the Change Healthcare home study tells its own credential story (Confirmed [132][42][36]). See [Cloud Attacks](/CLOUD_ATTACK_REFERENCE.md) and [Identity Security](/IDENTITY_SECURITY_REFERENCE.md).
- **Living off the land made dwell time long (Inferred).** Volt Typhoon's five-year footholds and the telecom campaign put detection weight on behavior, logging and configuration integrity rather than malware signatures (Confirmed [13][77]).
- **Ransomware ran as an affiliate economy that sold and bought access (Inferred).** Black Basta, Akira and RansomHub share the same entry set, RansomHub drew affiliates from LockBit and ALPHV, and Iranian actors sold access for a cut (Confirmed [39][36][60][59]). See [Ransomware Defense](/RANSOMWARE_DEFENSE_REFERENCE.md) and [the ransomware ecosystem study](/case-studies/RANSOMWARE_ECOSYSTEM.md).
- **Trust in maintainers and vendors was tested from three sides (Inferred).** Upstream XZ Utils releases were backdoored (Confirmed [29]), a domain sold in February 2024 was reported to serve malicious JavaScript through polyfill.io (Reported [47]), and a security vendor's own update crashed an estimated 8.5 million Windows devices (Confirmed [50][52]). See [Supply Chain Security](/SUPPLY_CHAIN_SECURITY.md).
- **Enforcement went after infrastructure and people, not only binaries (Inferred).** Cronos, Endgame and Magnus took servers and keys, and sanctions named leaders and front companies (Confirmed [93][40][72][19][28][79]). See [Threat Actors](/THREAT_ACTORS.md) and [Malware Families](/MALWARE_FAMILIES.md).

### What is overstated and what is understated

- **Overstated: "Snowflake was breached."** Mandiant found no evidence of a breach of Snowflake's enterprise environment; the opened record describes customer accounts accessed with infostealer credentials and no MFA, and AT&T's and Live Nation's filings say only "a third-party cloud" platform or database (Confirmed [42][44][43]; Inferred for the reading).
- **Overstated: the CrowdStrike outage as an attack.** CrowdStrike, CISA and Microsoft all describe a faulty update, not an intrusion (Confirmed [50][51][52]).
- **Overstated: firm national labels for some campaigns.** Mandiant declined to assess UNC5221's origin while Volexity "has reason to believe" UTA0178 is Chinese; Talos names no state for ArcaneDoor; the Electoral Commission compromise is attributed to "a Chinese state-affiliated entity", not to APT31; and the 2024 government statements on the telecom campaign do not use the name Salt Typhoon (Confirmed [84][2][91][27][75][77]).
- **Overstated: the Change Healthcare actor as a nation-state.** The first 8-K said "suspected nation-state associated"; the crime attribution came later and lives in the home study (Confirmed [21]).
- **Overstated: the precision of advisory technique tables.** Tables misfile tactics, AA24-109A prints a Mobile id in an Enterprise table, and revised live pages are not 2024 snapshots (Confirmed [36][39]; Demonstrated [146]; Inferred for the reading).
- **Overstated: dollar and victim figures read as totals.** LockBit's US$500 million is an alleged DOJ figure; "over 500" Black Basta victims, "at least 210" for RansomHub and "about 165 notified" for UNC5537 are lower bounds or notification counts; Akira's US$244.17 million is a 2025 figure (Confirmed [19][39][60][42][36]).
- **Understated: intrusions nobody detected.** Five-year dwell for Volt Typhoon and an integrity checker that missed compromise make the public record a floor (Confirmed [13][5]; Inferred for the reading).
- **Understated: ransomware losses.** IC3's US$12.47 million adjusted loss excludes downtime and remediation and covers only complaints to IC3, against Chainalysis's estimate of about US$813.55 million in payments, itself an on-chain lower bound (Confirmed [98]; Reported [99]; Inferred for the comparison).
- **Understated: routine OT tampering.** Hacktivist manipulation of water and other small ICS was common enough to draw a May fact sheet and a September alert, and the Unitronics defacements drew sanctions despite no disruption of critical services (Confirmed [118][119][12]).
- **Understated: the density of the government record.** Two emergency directives, a dozen sanctions actions, three named police operations, a product ban and binding standards in three jurisdictions fell in one year (Confirmed, Part 11; Inferred for the reading).
- **Understated: KEV's ransomware flag as history.** The flag is current, not the state at listing, so counts drawn from it can shift as CISA updates entries (Confirmed [3]; Inferred for the caution).

### Discrepancies in the record

- **Ivanti attribution.** Volexity "has reason to believe" UTA0178 is a Chinese nation-state-level actor; Mandiant, at publication, did not have enough data to assess UNC5221's origin (Confirmed [2][84]).
- **LockBit's scale.** The NCA counts more than 7,000 attacks built with LockBit's services from June 2022 to February 2024; Treasury, citing DOJ, says over 2,500 victims worldwide. They count different things (Confirmed [18][19]).
- **Cronos keys and affiliates.** The NCA's announcement says 1,000 decryption keys and 194 affiliates identified; its lead release says over 1,000 keys and 28 affiliate servers taken down (Confirmed [16][93]).
- **The Integrity Technology Group botnet.** The joint advisory counts over 260,000 devices as of June 2024; Lumen counts over 60,000 active at its June 2023 peak and more than 200,000 over time (Confirmed [82]; Reported [120]).
- **Fuxnet's reach.** Claroty estimates about 500 gateways; the group claims 2,659 (Confirmed [34]; Reported [34]).
- **Sichuan Silence's count.** One reading of the Treasury release gives "tens of thousands" of firewalls, another about 81,000 (Confirmed [79]).
- **Operation Endgame.** One reading of the Europol page lists Trickbot and the dates 27 to 29 May from its metadata; another saw no date and no Trickbot (Confirmed [40]).
- **The telecom guidance date.** CISA's alert is dated 3 December; the guidance page is dated 4 December (Confirmed [76][77]).
- **ED 24-02's date.** CISA's alert is dated 11 April; the directive page opened did not show an issue date and is now marked closed (Confirmed [10][116]).
- **CVE-2024-1709's weakness.** The CNA gives CWE-288; NVD gives NVD-CWE-Other (Confirmed [133]).
- **CVE-2024-6387's weakness.** The CNA gives CWE-364; NVD gives CWE-362 (Confirmed [137]).
- **CVE-2024-3400's weakness.** NVD gives CWE-77; the CNA gives CWE-20 and CWE-77 (Confirmed [134]).
- **The CSRB report's location.** The report is dated 20 March 2024 but is served from a 2025 path on cisa.gov (Confirmed [117]).
- **The moved Microsoft address.** The old msrc.microsoft.com address of the 19 January post redirects to the blog index; this study cites the copy at microsoft.com [7].
