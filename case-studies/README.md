# Case Studies

Evidence-graded studies of how the threat landscape is changing, written from primary sources: threat reports from AI providers and security vendors, government advisories, and the disclosures of the organizations that were hit. Each study is dated, because both the threats and the sources change.

Browse the timeline: [Case study timeline](/case-studies/TIMELINE.md) puts every study on one timeline with its dated key events, filterable by year, kind, sector, CVE and ATT&CK group.

## Start here

New here? These are good first reads, grouped by what you came for. The full, searchable table is below, and the [timeline](/case-studies/TIMELINE.md) filters every study by year, kind, sector, CVE and group.

**To understand a landmark intrusion**

- [SolarWinds and SUNBURST](/case-studies/SOLARWINDS_SUNBURST.md): a signed software update carried a backdoor into thousands of networks.
- [Stuxnet](/case-studies/STUXNET.md): malware that crossed an air gap and quietly changed what the centrifuges did.
- [Log4Shell](/case-studies/LOG4SHELL.md): one logging feature put remote code execution almost everywhere at once.

**To brief a leader on real-world impact**

- [The Change Healthcare attack of 2024](/case-studies/CHANGE_HEALTHCARE_2024.md): a single password without a second factor stopped claims and payments across US healthcare.
- [Colonial Pipeline and DarkSide](/case-studies/COLONIAL_DARKSIDE_2021.md): a ransomware hit on the business side took down fuel delivery to the US East Coast.
- [The ransomware ecosystem](/case-studies/RANSOMWARE_ECOSYSTEM.md): how extortion became an industry, with the players, the tradecraft and the money.

**To study a threat actor**

- [APT41: the public record](/case-studies/APT41_PUBLIC_RECORD.md): state espionage and crime for profit, told through the court record and vendor reporting.
- [Sandworm](/case-studies/SANDWORM_OPERATIONS.md): the GRU unit behind the Ukrainian blackouts and NotPetya, checked claim by claim.

**To follow a supply-chain attack**

- [The XZ Utils backdoor](/case-studies/XZ_UTILS_BACKDOOR_2024.md): a patient maintainer almost shipped an SSH backdoor to the whole Linux world.
- [npm supply-chain attacks](/case-studies/NPM_SUPPLY_CHAIN_ATTACKS.md): years of malicious packages on the public registry, and the defenses that answer them.

**To see where the threat is heading**

- [AI threats in the cybersecurity industry](/case-studies/AI_THREATS_2026.md): real cases of AI used in intrusions, deepfakes and malware, with a defensive playbook.

## Method

Every study follows the same rules.

1. **Primary sources first.** Facts come from the report, advisory, or disclosure that first published them, linked to the exact page. Where only press coverage was available, the study says so.
2. **Every event carries an evidence grade.** The grade separates what has happened from what has only been shown to be possible.
3. **Hype and evidence are kept apart.** Each study says which threats are overstated and which are understated, and why.
4. **Numbers are reported as the source states them.** A study notes when a figure is a floor, an estimate, or a vendor's own count.
5. **Figures are checked against their sources.** Every figure in a study is checked against the source it cites.
6. **Every study is on the timeline.** Every new study adds its entry to [data/case_studies.yaml](https://github.com/TeamStarWolf/TeamStarWolf/blob/main/data/case_studies.yaml) (kind, period, sectors, CVEs, ATT&CK groups and up to 12 dated events from its own timeline) and reruns `scripts/build_case_study_timeline.py`; the check in CI fails otherwise.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confirmed | An observed, attributed fact stated in a primary source opened for the study: the victim or affected organization, a government agency, the AI provider, the KEV feed, or the library's own data. A party's account of its own investigation is Confirmed as that party's statement |
| Reported | A single vendor's account of in-the-wild activity, or a secondary account such as journalism or an analyst note, not independently corroborated |
| Inferred | This study's own reasoning from the cited facts, including conclusions drawn from absence, labelled where it appears |
| Demonstrated | A research proof of concept, a fixed vulnerability with no known in-the-wild use, or a check the library ran in a lab |

Grades are applied inline, next to each claim, in the body of every study. Many studies carry their own version of this table, headed "Meaning in this study" and tuned to the sources they opened, and the slide decks gather a section's grades into a single "Grades:" line.

## About the figures

Figures are original charts and diagrams drawn from the data each study cites. Each figure's caption names its source. The how-to screenshots in the CVE-2026-98432 and Multipath TCP studies are renderings of real command output from a lab host running Kali 2026.2 with kernel 7.1.5, captured on 3 November 2026 with a neutral prompt. The screenshots in the npm supply-chain study are renderings of real command output from a throwaway `node:22.11.0-bookworm-slim` container on a lab host (npm 10.9.0), captured on 4 November 2026 UTC with the same neutral prompt; one has rows cut that carried package authors' contact details, and the study notes the cut. ## About the downloads

Studies are exported to PDF, and to a slide deck in PowerPoint format once a slide plan is written for them. Both are generated from the study's Markdown by `scripts/export_case_study.py`: `pdf` writes one study's PDF to `case-studies/pdf`, `all-pdf` writes the PDF of every study in the table above that lacks one, and `pptx` renders a slide plan from `case-studies/slides/plans` to `case-studies/slides`.
