## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Several kinds of change, no single story

- Email campaigns made the ransomware visible at scale; reporting described encryption, threatened disclosure and threatened denial of service.
- Researchers identified a conditional recovery opportunity, and later public tools changed the options for victims.
- No single development establishes that every organization recovered, every stolen copy disappeared or every participant stopped.
- The study avoids a single definitive launch date and avoids combining message volume, victim listings and released keys into a fictitious total.

## PART 1 | A campaign count is not a victim count

- Proofpoint's June 25, 2020 research described more than one million Avaddon messages between June 4 and June 10, over 750,000 on June 6.
- The June 4 campaign focused on transportation and school districts; other reported targeting included manufacturing, education and media.
- A message can be blocked, ignored, investigated or executed; the scale shows delivery pressure within the vendor's visibility.
- A large numeral labeled simply "victims" would change the source's meaning, so volume stays attached to the window and the vendor.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported, vendor-observed messages.
Notes: Campaign window, counts and targeting from [1]; no conversion rate to infections is invented.

## PART 1 | Research recovery was conditional, not a break of encryption

- Yuste and Pastrana's February 2021 paper demonstrated a memory-based recovery approach under essential conditions.
- Key material had to remain in memory or a captured image, and the evaluation used an original version of an encrypted file.
- The findings concern key handling and implementation, not a general defeat of AES or RSA.
- The approach created a time-sensitive forensic issue; a dated experiment cannot be assumed to work against every later build.
Caption: Evidence grade: Demonstrated under stated conditions.
Notes: Conditions, tested January 2021 sample and controlled environment from [4]; MedusaLocker similarity kept as suggestive, not identity.

## PART 2 | A reported shutdown established less than the word implies

- NUKIB reported on June 16, 2021 that the operation had ended on June 11, with the operator site unavailable and the reason unknown.
- The notice attributed the initial key-release reporting to BleepingComputer and pointed victims to Emsisoft's tool.
- Published institution listings and a larger key-derived estimate are not interchangeable measurements.
- The account supports a reported shutdown; it does not prove an arrest, prosecution, permanent exit or erasure of stolen data.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported shutdown; cause unknown in the account.
Notes: Shutdown report, relayed attribution and contrasted counts from [5]; no victim census, payment rate or revenue is inferred.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Separate the pressures before choosing a response

- Encryption threatens availability, unauthorized copying threatens confidentiality and flooding threatens reachability.
- A practical incident board assigns an owner and an evidence question to each workstream.
- A message promising a DDoS is evidence of a threat, not evidence that a flood is underway.
- An outage should not be dismissed because a claim is unverified; inspect the service and keep claim separate from measurement.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred; original defensive analysis.
Notes: Separation model and the claim-versus-measurement point are original analysis in the study's operational sections.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Email volume should lead to exposure analysis

- Model recipients as states: rejected, delivered, accessed, attachment retrieved, execution observed and further activity confirmed.
- Define the denominator before reporting percentages; mixing messages, accounts and devices distorts a control's apparent strength.
- A campaign search locates related messages but should not label every recipient compromised.
- Keep remote access as a separate investigation branch; phishing in a mailbox does not prove it caused the intrusion.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred investigative guidance.
Notes: Denominator discipline and the separate remote-access branch from the study's response sections; "affected" is kept distinct from "compromised".

## DEFENDER VIEW | The preservation dilemma is a decision, not a slogan

- Preserving volatile evidence may retain a recovery option; letting an infected environment run may cause further harm.
- A mature plan names who assesses active harm, who preserves evidence and who accepts the business consequence, before an incident.
- The record should capture decision time, observed state, options and rationale, honestly noting when evidence could not be preserved.
- Preserved memory and disk evidence is sensitive; a recovery possibility is not permission to upload an image to an arbitrary service.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred; a decision framework, not a universal instruction.
Notes: Preservation-versus-shutdown tension and handling limits from the study; it never directs an untrained user to improvise live malware handling.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Distinguish mechanisms before making a promise

- A public decryptor can exist from an implementation weakness, a key-release event or vendor support for certain formats only.
- Preserve the chronology: a newer tool does not invalidate an earlier limitation, and an old limitation does not bar a newer tool.
- Backups and decryptors are complementary; compare data age, copy trust, dependencies and validation cost without treating either as a guarantee.
- Acceptance criteria should reflect business meaning; a file that opens may still hold damaged records.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred recovery-workflow guidance.
Notes: Mechanism distinction, chronology and backup comparison from the study; it makes no claim to have run a tool or verified a success rate.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What this case overstates versus understates

Left: Easy to overstate
- A million messages read as a million completed incidents.
- A reported shutdown read as arrest, eradication or a confirmed rebrand.
- A tool's existence read as successful recovery of all affected data.
Right: Easy to understate
- Three distinct pressures that a single technical fix does not resolve.
- A conditional, dated recovery method with real prerequisites.
- Conflicting origin dates that remain unresolved in the record.
Caption: Evidence grade: Mixed; each claim bounded to its source.
Notes: Overstated and understated contrasts synthesized across the study; "claimed by operators" stays separate from "confirmed".

## CLOSE | Disciplined separation makes the lessons usable

- Distinguish messages from infections and keys from organizations.
- Distinguish threatened harm from observed harm, and recovery tools from successful recovery.
- Distinguish a reported shutdown from eradication, and an RaaS affiliate structure from one named actor.
- These distinctions do not weaken a case study; they make its recommendations usable when a real organization must decide what to do next.
Caption: Evidence grade: case-specific conclusions, outcome claims bounded.
Notes: Conclusion synthesis across [1] through [8]; not-verified items include a complete victim census, the shutdown cause and a definitive operator identity.
