- The record separates protected password records, recovered passwords and account entry.
- Most first-party evidence sits in one evolving company FAQ, so the account stays bounded.
- The contribution is an evidence comparison and an audit of communicating residual risk.

## KEY FACTS | Three populations, three different meanings

Stat: 139M | Profiles exposed, company upper bound
Stat: 137.3M | Affected addresses HIBP displays
Stat: ~4M | Accounts with recovered passwords
- Canva reported interrupting the attack on May 24, 2019, and reset active pre-breach tokens.
- The June update described bcrypt-protected passwords for password logins.
- On January 11, 2020 Canva learned of about four million accounts with recovered passwords.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed and Reported figures; units differ.
Notes: Upper-bound profiles and reset from [1]; the 137.3 million affected-address figure is HIBP's collection descriptor, not a takeover count [2].

## COUNTING | Keep the unit attached to every figure

- Company upper-bound profiles, HIBP affected addresses and recovered passwords are different units.
- A social-login account can appear in profile information without the same kind of password record.
- Subtracting the figures does not reveal takeovers, deduplication or an initial exaggeration.
- An address in a breach record does not demonstrate that its mailbox was entered.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported figures; not interchangeable counts.
Notes: Profile and HIBP descriptors from [1][2]; each possible explanation of the difference would require evidence and none follows from arithmetic alone.

## HASHING | A recovered hash is not a recovered password

- The record describes bcrypt-protected passwords, a one-way verifier, not reversible ciphertext.
- "Decrypted" implies a broken key or algorithm; "recovered" or "cracked" fits password hashes.
- Protection at rest makes recovery harder, yet about four million passwords were still recovered.
- An exposed hash is not an exposed password, and a copied verifier escapes login throttling.
Caption: Evidence grade: Demonstrated mechanism; no algorithm break claimed.
Notes: One-way verification and adaptable cost from [3]; the study does not claim Canva's algorithm was cryptographically broken, nor infer attacker hardware or work rate [1].

## EXTERNAL ALERTS | One upstream source, two delivery channels

- Mozilla Monitor records the May 24, 2019 breach and August 9, 2019 addition, sourced from HIBP.
- Mozilla and HIBP are therefore not two independent forensic confirmations of the collection.
- A later monitoring alert is another delivery channel, not evidence of a later intrusion.
- A notice sent is not the same as understanding achieved or action completed.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed records; one upstream source.
Notes: Mozilla's dates and explicit HIBP dependency from [4]; the reviewed record supplies no complete notification or password-change funnel, so none is invented.

## INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE | Reuse is the cross-service risk

- The UP IT Development Center issued a Canva advisory on August 13, 2019 for UP Mail users.
- Miami University published a further advisory on February 28, 2020 for students and faculty.
- Neither notice proves a university's own infrastructure was breached through Canva.
- The pathway is a reused secret; a provider reset cannot replace the same password elsewhere.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed advisories; no secondary compromise shown.
Notes: UP advisory from [5]; Miami advisory from [6], using its original publication date rather than its later maintenance timestamp.

## BEHAVIORAL RESEARCH | Framing question, not a victim rate

- Bhagavatula, Bauer and Kapadia studied 249 participants during 2017-2018, before Canva.
- They found limited constructive response and incomplete changes to similar passwords.
- This frames an evaluation question; it is not a measurement of Canva users.
- The reviewed record lacks the data to compute any Canva password-change completion rate.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported research; pre-Canva, contextual only.
Notes: Sample, dates and findings from [7]; the useful question is whether the response reduced still-valid historical secrets, which the public record cannot answer.

## EDITORIAL AUDIT | Proposed wording that keeps the action visible

These are original proposed edits for the account, not quotations from Canva.

| Vague wording | More precise replacement |
|---|---|
| Broad password reassurance | Protection makes recovery harder; an unchanged password still needs changing |
| An unexplained single count | Upper-bound profiles and affected addresses are not interchangeable |
| Universal no-impact claim | No evidence of the specific activity described, as of that update |
| New-breach implication | New evidence about old stolen data triggered another protective action |
| Proof of safety from a reset | The reset is a precaution; entry requires separate evidence |
Caption: Evidence grade: Original proposed edits, not Canva quotations.
Notes: Replacement wordings from [1]; each keeps the protective value of hashing while making the required action and the negative finding's scope visible.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Keep affected, claimed and confirmed apart

- Keep "affected" (present in the dump) apart from "compromised" (an account actually entered).
- Keep the attacker's OAuth-token claim apart from what Canva confirmed it had evidence of.
- Keep accounts present in the collection apart from passwords actually recovered.
- Keep the breach date, the public-release date and the disclosure updates on one timeline.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred investigative discipline from the record.
Notes: Claim-versus-confirmation and token caveats from [1]; collection descriptors from [2]. Lack of evidence of one access type is not a guarantee about every consequence.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What the retelling overstates vs understates

Left: Easy to overstate
- Reading 139 million as confirmed account takeovers.
- Treating an exposed bcrypt hash as an exposed password.
- A later alert or reset as proof of a new January intrusion.
Right: Easy to understate
- About four million passwords were in fact recovered.
- Reused credentials carry the risk into unrelated accounts.
- A difficult-to-recover password can still be a potentially exposed one.
Caption: Evidence grade: Editorial corrections the case requires.
Notes: Overstated and understated contrasts from [1][2][3]; keep affected apart from compromised and the recovered-password population apart from the profile population.

## CLOSE | A source-bounded account beats a reassuring headline

- A technically correct explanation can still deliver an overly reassuring message.
- Access to protected records, recovery of passwords and account entry are three claims.
- Those distinctions should guide the response, not obscure the action a user must take.
- A shorter, source-bounded account outperforms a generic checklist or an invented victim total.
