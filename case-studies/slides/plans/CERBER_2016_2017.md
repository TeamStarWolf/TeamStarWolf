## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | A family label organizes research but cannot supply scope

- Cerber shows how one malware family can support many campaigns without those campaigns being a single intrusion.
- A count of campaigns is not a count of criminals; a file-encryption observation does not establish every consequence.
- A researcher's recovery breakthrough does not guarantee that an old tool works today.
- No malware, portal, leaked data or attacker infrastructure was accessed or executed for this study.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported history plus original defensive analysis.
Notes: Executive-assessment framing; five primary records are [1] through [5], described without any deployment recipe.

## PART 1 | Four distinct objects keep names from implying facts

- Maintain four separate objects: the malware sample, the distribution campaign, the affected organization, and the economic activity.
- Record relationships with their evidence instead of assuming them from a shared name.
- A sample record preserves collection time, provenance and the observations behind classification.
- A campaign search is an investigative expansion, not an automatic declaration that every matching user is compromised.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred analytical model from the historical distinctions.
Notes: Original analytical model, not a reconstruction of private victim logs; grounded in the study's four-object section.

## PART 1 | Affiliate structure limits what an actor name can carry

- An affiliate model lets shared software coexist with different delivery, victim selection and operational discipline.
- Keep "claimed by operators" separate from "confirmed", and affiliate roles separate from one named actor.
- State confidence in the delivery chain rather than forcing an early actor name or nationality.
- Language, hosting location and victim distribution answer different questions; none alone establishes state sponsorship.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred; the study makes no state-attribution claim.
Notes: Role-based description over a single group label; development, distribution and payment handling may involve different participants [1][4].

## PART 2 | The recovery service was a narrow window, not a broken cipher

- Check Point researchers describe a server-side validation flaw that enabled a temporary recovery service.
- The paper reports that thousands of users recovered files while the service operated.
- This is a primary report of the researchers' own work, not evidence that every infection was recoverable.
- It does not establish that the underlying cryptographic algorithms were universally broken.
Caption: Evidence grade: Demonstrated under stated conditions, not universal.
Notes: Skuratovich and Rona via Virus Bulletin [2]; exploit implementation details are omitted and no old system is reconnected to historical service addresses.

## PART 2 | Delivery and host behavior accumulated over time

- Microsoft records email and exploit-kit distribution, script-based downloaders and Office content, with password-protected attachments by October 2016.
- The entry describes RSA and RC4 use, changing extensions, a spoken ransom message, persistence, and shadow-copy deletion.
- These are family observations over time; they do not mean every sample performed every listed action.
- Unit 42's Blank Slate campaign used empty-body messages with nested archives and also delivered other ransomware.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported family and campaign observations.
Notes: Behavior catalogue from [3]; the February 2017 example, shifting hosting and the separate-campaign point from [4]; paying is not stated to guarantee file access.

## PART 2 | The 2017 changes were particular, not universal

- Trend Micro describes a March 2017 loader change apparently aimed at pre-execution machine-learning detection.
- It reports an August variant that stole cryptocurrency wallet files and saved browser passwords.
- A loader challenging one detection class does not prove all machine-learning products failed.
- Observed theft capability in one variant does not mean every Cerber infection included credential theft.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported retrospective findings, variant-specific.
Notes: Only the relevant Cerber passages in the annual report were reviewed [5]; two underlying article pages did not yield usable text, so the report itself is cited.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Reconstruct the delivery chain without user blame

- Reconstruct the chain as observable transitions: receipt, attachment access, extraction, active-content execution, payload retrieval, file modification.
- A mailbox search can support receipt; it cannot alone prove the later transitions.
- Treat user interviews as context, not perfect event logs; ask neutral questions and compare with technical records.
- Finding a suspicious message does not prove it caused the observed encryption; align timestamps and process relationships.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred defensive guidance (a fictional incident).
Notes: Original delivery-chain analysis; controls should reduce the consequential decisions a user must make, with owned, expiring exceptions.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Detection should record what the tool actually did

- A detection label is an interpretation; a prevention result is an outcome at a particular stage.
- Reports should say when the alert appeared, what the product claims to have prevented, and what independent evidence confirms.
- A family name alone is weak support for keeping a host isolated.
- Record execution status, affected paths, persistence, accessible shares and restoration readiness instead.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred review discipline from the sources.
Notes: Safe evaluation can use approved benign simulations with a defined owner and rollback; historical ransomware samples are unnecessary for many questions.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Decryption and recovery require qualification

- A historical recovery success can justify consulting reputable current resources, not a promise to the affected organization.
- Preserve the original encrypted material and work from copies; record tool version, source, inputs and outcome.
- A small successful test supports staged expansion, not immediate replacement of all originals.
- Payment and technical recovery are separate decisions; a demand does not prove a working recovery mechanism exists.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred recovery guidance, bounded to this case.
Notes: Applicability depends on the actual variant, evidence, provenance and requirements; uploaded samples may leave the organization's control, so recipient and purpose are assessed first.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Confidentiality remains a separate investigation

- Successful restoration of documents does not settle whether credentials or other information were exposed.
- Ask what the observed payload could access and what evidence exists of collection or transmission.
- Avoid both assuming theft without evidence and dismissing it because the visible symptom was encryption.
- A cryptocurrency file-theft capability does not itself prove that assets moved; that needs further evidence.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred; capability is not a fabricated loss figure.
Notes: Ties to the 2017 theft observations [5]; distinguish password changes from session or token invalidation, following scope rather than a single platform procedure.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What this case overstates versus understates

Left: Easy to overstate
- Reading 161 campaigns as 161 criminals, or a month's snapshot as a lifetime census.
- Treating a historical free decryptor as a current universal guarantee.
- Converting a criminal demand or a research projection into verified financial loss.
Right: Easy to understate
- A real affiliate economy with distinct developer and distributor roles.
- A genuine but narrow recovery window that attackers closed within about 24 hours.
- Multiple delivery routes and changing variants under one familiar label.
Caption: Evidence grade: Mixed; each claim bounded to its source's scope.
Notes: Keep demand, payment, traced receipt and estimated profit as different quantities [1][2][3][4][5]; the label organizes research but cannot supply incident scope.

## CLOSE | Precision beats a fabricated victim narrative

- A familiar malware name can connect research, but it cannot supply missing evidence about a particular organization.
- Preserve the date, scope and uncertainty of each claim while still supporting action.
- Keep exposure separate from execution, affected separate from compromised, and claimed separate from confirmed.
- Open gaps: no lifetime census, no definitive actor identity, no presently guaranteed universal decryptor.
Caption: Evidence grade: Case-specific conclusions, outcome claims bounded.
Notes: Conclusion synthesis across [1] through [5]; recovery becomes more reliable when these distinctions stay visible to technical and business decision-makers.
