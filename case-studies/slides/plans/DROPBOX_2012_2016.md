## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Dropbox is about the long life of stolen credentials

- The 2012 incident and the 2016 collection are different events [1][2].
- Treating the later disclosure as a new intrusion obscures the real lesson.
- Keep each intrusion method attached to its correct victim [1].
- An exposed credential collection is not proof every file account was opened.

## PART 1 | What the 2012 statement actually established

- Reused credentials from other sites entered a small number of accounts [1].
- A stolen password reached an employee document holding user email addresses [1].
- The company announced detection measures and planned two-factor authentication [1].
- A feature announced as coming is not a control already protecting accounts.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed contemporary company statement.
Notes: The 31 July 2012 findings and announced features from [1], read at their own scope.

## PART 2 | The 2016 surfacing was not a fresh intrusion

- The August 2016 post confirmed the list was genuine and likely from 2012 [2].
- It reset accounts whose password was unchanged since mid-2012 [2].
- "No indication of improper account access" is a bounded finding, not a guarantee [2].
- A collection becoming visible is not a second intrusion into the service.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed update; negative finding bounded.
Notes: The 25 August post and 31 August update, reset scope and dated findings from [2].

## PART 3 | Approximately 68 million accounts, not people

- The 2021 filing cites credentials for approximately 68 million accounts [3].
- An accounts count is not a count of distinct people or active users [3].
- A person or organization can hold several accounts or dormant identities.
- Keep the approximation and the word accounts; do not invent a victim total.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported filing figure; an approximation.
Notes: Scale, units and their limits from [3]; cracked-password and deduplicated counts are not established.

## PART 4 | An exposed hash is not a recovered password

- The 2021 filing cites emails and hashed, salted passwords [3].
- Hashing and salting make recovery harder, not impossible [3].
- The study does not assign a per-account algorithm breakdown [3].
- A live-store upgrade does not erase bytes an attacker already copied.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed hashing and salting; no algorithm breakdown.
Notes: Password-store and historical-migration discussion from [3]; a protected verifier stays in the exposure assessment without being a readable password.

## PART 5 | Credential exposure is not proof of file theft

- The record establishes credential exposure and dated access findings [1-3].
- It does not establish mass theft of every account's file content.
- Separate authentication evidence from content-access evidence.
- A notice can say replace the old secret without declaring files were opened.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed credential exposure; no mass file theft.
Notes: Content-versus-credential distinction from [1-3]; shared-folder relationships are a preventive analysis, not a Dropbox finding.

## DEFENDER VIEW | A reset is one secret, not every form of access

- A password reset changes one authentication secret, nothing more.
- Sessions, devices and application grants need separate, explicit treatment.
- Model password, session, device and integration state with an owner each.
- Test with a synthetic pre-reset session before millions of customers ask.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred defensive analysis for a comparable provider.
Notes: State model and testing guidance drawn from the study's reset-and-revocation section; original analysis anchored to [2].

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What the retelling overstates and understates

Left: Easy to overstate
- A 2016 "new breach": the data was taken in 2012.
- Exposed hashes read as cracked passwords.
- A credential dump treated as proof files were stolen.
Right: Easy to understate
- Old data stays operationally relevant for years.
- A reset clears one secret, not sessions or devices.
- Reused passwords sit outside the service's control.
Caption: Evidence grade: editorial corrections the case requires.
Notes: Overstated and understated contrasts drawn across [1], [2] and [3]; keep dates, units and remediation scope separate.

## CLOSE | Evidence, account state and customer action

- Old stolen data can stay operationally relevant long after the intrusion.
- A good response revises a historical understanding without inventing an event.
- Keep incident, disclosure and later legal dates on separate tracks.
- The lesson is a disciplined link between evidence, state and customer action.
