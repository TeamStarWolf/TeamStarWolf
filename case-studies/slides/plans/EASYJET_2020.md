## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Two connected histories, not one attributable breach

- The easyJet case is a 2020 customer-data incident and a much longer record of notification, regulation and civil exposure.
- A count of affected customers captures scale, not the duration or meaning of the aftermath.
- The record does not establish an initial-access technique, a named perpetrator or a complete intrusion timeline.
- Closure of an investigation is not a finding that the company's security was adequate.

Notes: Executive-assessment framing from [1] and the regulator record [7][8][9]; no exploit, vulnerability id or perpetrator established.

## KEY FACTS | Exposure scale, a card subset, and a long aftermath

Stat: ~9M | Customers' email & travel data accessed

- The 19 May 2020 announcement reported access to email and travel data for approximately nine million customers. [1]
- The 2,208 card-detail customers are a subset of that population, not an additional count, and not nine million stolen cards. [1]
- The card group was notified in April 2020 and the wider email population in May. [3]
- ICO investigation INV/0250/2020 was closed on 17 August 2023. [7]

## PART 1 | The disclosure separates access from later misuse

- The announcement described forensic support, NCSC and ICO notification, terminated access, and no evidence of misuse at that point. [1]
- Access concerns what the intruder reached; misuse concerns what happened afterwards, a different evidentiary question.
- A contemporaneous "no misuse" statement is not a guarantee about later activity outside the company's environment.
- The company's "sophisticated" description is its characterisation, not a technical explanation of the attack.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported company representations, not an independent forensic report.
Notes: Access-versus-misuse separation and the "sophisticated" caveat from [1].

## PART 1 | Response communication is itself an evidence problem

- There were two tasks: reaching the card-detail customers and reaching the much wider travel-data group. [1]
- "Notifications sent" and "customers successfully informed" are not synonymous; the public record shows no delivery receipts.
- Travel data can make an impersonation message more believable, an observation about the data, not a count of victims.
- A good notice places both together: what is known about exposure, what is not established about later activity, and what to do.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred communication-audit guidance drawn from the disclosure.
Notes: Audience split and the phishing-precaution linkage from [1]; no documented downstream fraud total exists.

## PART 2 | Pandemic disruption is not invented cyber impact

- The June 2020 half-year report separates pandemic effects from the data-breach issue. [4]
- Fleet grounding from 30 March is attributed to travel restrictions, not to the attack. [4]
- Home-working risk and authentication upgrades do not establish that those controls failed in the incident. [4]
- Do not treat all 2020 airline losses as cyber losses, or every security upgrade that year as proven breach remediation.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported company disclosure; the causal caution is analysis.
Notes: Separation of COVID-19 and breach risks, grounding cause and remediation caveats from [4].

## PART 2 | From incident to continuing financial disclosure

- The May 2021 interim report records continuing ICO discussions and no recognised provision for the matter. [5]
- No provision does not mean nothing was spent, that there is no future liability, or that claims were rejected.
- Cost buckets stay separate: response, notification, legal defence, penalties, settlements and broader commercial effect.
- The reviewed records do not establish a complete total across those buckets.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported dated contingent-liability position.
Notes: No-provision reading and cost-bucket discipline from [5].

## PART 2 | The investigation was still live in May 2023

- The May 2023 interim report described an ongoing ICO investigation, a UK group claim and claims commenced or threatened elsewhere. [6]
- The company expected the ICO to act as lead supervisory authority, a company description, not an adjudicated ruling. [6]
- "Latest source" and "best source" differ: a later filing can summarise closure, but the regulator letter is stronger on its own reasoning.
- Sources combine by function: company account, regulator decision, claimant position.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported company snapshot shortly before closure.
Notes: Live-investigation status and the jurisdictional expectation from [6].

## PART 2 | What the August 2023 closure letter actually says

- The ICO letter dated 17 August 2023 identifies investigation INV/0250/2020 and is signed by Deputy Commissioner Stephen Bonner. [7]
- The case was de-prioritised through administrative discretion, since enforcement was not judged the best use of limited resources. [7]
- This is a prioritisation decision, not a finding of full compliance, an unavoidable attack, or no customer harm.
- It is also not proof of a secret arrangement; the public document explains a resource-based decision.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed original letter; a stated rationale, not a merits finding.
Notes: Closure rationale, signatory and case identifier from [7].

## PART 2 | FOI responses add process, not a hidden attack chain

- The 1 December 2023 FOI response names Bonner as decision-maker under delegated powers. [8]
- It distinguishes material withheld under legal-advice privilege from a supplied nonprivileged email exchange, and records redactions. [8]
- Privileged advice is not evidence of wrongdoing, and a redacted name does not prove operational responsibility for the incident.
- An evidence register should separate: read, identified but not inspected, withheld by the source, and not located.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed FOI response read in full; a referenced email exchange not independently inspected.
Notes: Delegation, privilege and redaction distinctions from [8].

## PART 2 | A date discrepancy handled by keeping the evidence

- The 7 August 2025 response says the ICO held no agreement with easyJet ending the investigation. [9]
- It could not calculate investigation costs from the available staff-time data, which is not the same as zero cost. [9]
- That response refers to a closure letter dated 17 July 2023, differing from the original 17 August date. [7][9]
- The study keeps 17 August, tied to the original document, and records the discrepancy rather than inventing a July decision.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed FOI response; a dated conflict kept visible, not reconciled by invention.
Notes: No-agreement wording, the cost-calculation limit and the July versus August discrepancy from [7][9].

## PART 2 | Regulatory closure did not finish the civil story

- The 2025 annual report still described group actions and other claims while reporting the ICO investigation closed. [10]
- The company said the likely outcome and amount remained uncertain and included no provision. [10]
- An early intention to seek a group litigation order is not evidence that an order was granted. [2]
- Regulatory and civil tracks have different functions; one institution can close while another process continues.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported dated disclosure, not a verified final disposition.
Notes: Contingent-liability note from [10]; the GLO-intention caveat from [2].

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | Where the record is overstated versus understated

Left: Easy to overstate
- "Nine million credit cards stolen": the card group is a smaller subset.
- "The attack grounded the airline": grounding was pandemic-related.
- "The ICO cleared easyJet": the regulator closed the case on priorities.
Right: Easy to understate
- A broad travel-data exposure that can support impersonation risk.
- A long aftermath of notification, disclosure and civil uncertainty.
- A closure that can leave less public detail than an enforcement notice.
Caption: Evidence grade: editorial corrections the case requires.
Notes: Corrections and contrasts from [1][4][7]; keep exposure, misuse and closure distinct.

## CLOSE | Aftermath deserves as much care as disclosure

- The most substantial public evidence concerns how different institutions described and handled uncertainty over time.
- Preserve the distinctions: exposure versus misuse, booking periods versus intrusion periods, statements versus findings.
- Keep closure separate from exoneration, and civil allegations separate from adjudicated outcomes.
- A case study is stronger when it refuses to make the technical record look more complete than it is.
Caption: Evidence grade: case-specific conclusions, outcome claims bounded.
Notes: Conclusion synthesis across [1] through [10]; precision over an invented victim narrative.
