# Slide plan: eBay 2014: Employee Credentials and Marketplace Recovery

- Study: case-studies/EBAY_2014.md
- Cover title: eBay 2014 / Employee Credentials / Customer Password Recovery
- Subtitle: Employee credentials opened a corporate network holding an encrypted-password user store; recovery and credential reuse were the real risks.
- Published: 18 November 2026
- Notes: The cover sets the frame: a corporate access boundary preceded a marketplace-wide password reset.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Two identity problems, one marketplace response

- An attacker used compromised employee credentials to enter a corporate environment holding customer data.
- The company described a compromised authentication database and a broad password-change request.
- Protecting staff access and helping customers recover were related tasks needing different controls and owners.
- Describing data as non-financial, or passwords as encrypted, does not make either harmless or guaranteed.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed company disclosure; defensive analysis is original.
Notes: Executive-assessment framing from [1] and [2]; recommendations are analysis for a comparable marketplace, not findings that eBay lacked a control.

## KEY FACTS | Scale and scope the record actually supports

Stat: 145M | Active buyers, end of Q1 2014
Stat: 6 | Exposed profile data categories
Stat: 0 | Financial or card access found
- The 21 May 2014 disclosure reported a small number of compromised employee login credentials. [1]
- Exposed categories were names, protected passwords, email and physical addresses, phone numbers, birth dates. [1]
- A 2024 filing retrospectively described records of up to 145 million users, an upper bound. [5]
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, company announcement and filings.
Notes: Category list and the dated financial-data exclusion from [1]; the 145 million active-buyer figure from [2]; the retrospective upper bound from [5].

## PART 1 | A credential names who; authorization sets reach

- Employee credentials were a reported access mechanism, not a complete attack graph.
- The sources do not establish phishing, malware, reuse or insider as the acquisition route.
- A credential establishes who a system believes is connecting; authorization sets which records it reaches.
- Resetting one password can leave sessions, delegated access or a second identity untouched.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed sequence; acquisition method not established.
Notes: The credential-versus-authorization distinction and the incomplete-remediation risk from [1]; the study states the gaps rather than filling them with a familiar breach narrative.

## PART 1 | Keep activity, discovery and disclosure apart

Each milestone is a separate fact; the right column marks what it does not prove.

| Milestone | Supported finding | Does not establish |

| Late Feb to early Mar 2014 | Database compromise window | An exact day or full scope |
| Early May 2014 | Corporate access discovered | Immediate full understanding |
| May 21, 2014 | Disclosure and reset request | Every user actually reset |
| May 23, 2014 | UK reuse and multi-account notice | Other sites were breached |
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed dated milestones; non-claims kept explicit.
Notes: Chronology and detection-interval caution from [2]; disclosure milestones from [1] and [3]; an approximate interval must not become an exact timestamp.

## PART 1 | A database population is not a victim count

- Active buyers, accounts, database records and individual people are different units.
- A person may hold several accounts; an account can persist in a store while commercially inactive.
- A request directed to all users is broader than exposure confirmed through record analysis.
- A reset-completion rate is meaningless until its denominator is stated.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed figures; unit distinctions are analysis.
Notes: The all-users request alongside 145 million active buyers from [2]; the retrospective up-to-145-million records formulation from [5]; preserve the noun attached to each number.

## PART 2 | Encrypted is not an algorithm or a guarantee

- The company used the term encrypted passwords in its public descriptions. [1]
- The sources do not establish an algorithm, salt design, work factor or key management.
- This study assigns no cracking duration and does not claim every password became readable.
- A login service can rate-limit requests; a copied verifier dataset is attacked unseen.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed wording; technical specifics not established.
Notes: The encrypted-password wording from [1]; current salted-hashing and evidence-driven-change guidance from [7] is a modern reference point, not a retroactive finding about 2014 eBay controls.

## PART 2 | Non-financial data is not harmless data

- A birth date does not rotate like a secret, and an address can remain relevant for years.
- A phone number can be tied to account recovery and support verification.
- A consolidated record can support convincing impersonation even without card data.
- Help desks that treat exposed profile facts as identity proof can be undermined after a reset.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred risk assessment, not a claim each misuse occurred.
Notes: Persistence of non-financial attributes and the recovery-abuse risk from the study's section 5; classify information by what a combined record enables, not by whether one field is public.

## PART 2 | A stolen credential meets the access it is granted

- A single stolen credential becomes a large breach only where the environment permits broad access.
- Start from a task-based inventory: who inspects one record versus who can export a population.
- Stronger authentication reduces some credential risk but cannot correct excessive permissions.
- Log meaningful actions, including export account, dataset, volume and destination, not just logins.
Caption: Evidence grade: Original defensive analysis for a comparable marketplace.
Notes: Access-architecture framing from the study's section 6; the reported employee-credential pathway comes from [1]; evidence collection must not create a new sensitive store with broader access.

## PART 2 | A broad reset is an operation, not a message

- Customers arrive through mobile apps, browsers, localized sites and recovery flows.
- The May 23 UK notice told users to change each account and any reused password elsewhere. [3]
- Changing a reused secret is protective; it is not proof another service was breached.
- Define completion as a verified state change, never as an email sent.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed notice; reuse guidance distinguished from breach.
Notes: Multiple-account and reuse advice from [3]; measure delivery delay, failed attempts and recovery escalations by channel rather than a single global average.

## PART 2 | Recognized costs are not lifetime loss

Stat: $46M | Cyberattack expenses and credits
Stat: $41M | Of which, within Marketplaces
- The 2014 Form 10-K recorded about $46 million in cyberattack-related expenses and customer credits. [4]
- It named investigation, remediation, support, enhanced protection and marketing among categories. [4]
- The filing said lost revenue directly attributable to the attack could not be measured precisely. [4]
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed accounting categories, not a lifetime estimate.
Notes: Reported amounts, the seller-credit revenue reduction and the measurement limitation from [4]; keep recognized costs, estimated commercial effects and hypothetical long-term consequences in separate columns.

## PART 2 | The PayPal boundary was dated and scoped

- The May announcement reported no evidence of unauthorized access to PayPal data. [1]
- It described PayPal information as residing separately on a secure network. [1]
- That was a dated finding about one part of the environment, not permanent isolation of every brand.
- A segmentation claim is stronger when investigators can name the paths and logs examined.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed dated finding; not a universal isolation guarantee.
Notes: The PayPal exclusion and separate-network description from [1]; technical isolation and user password reuse are different issues and should not be merged in customer messaging.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Assess trust boundaries, invent no intrusion

- The public sources support credential compromise and database exposure, not an exploit recipe.
- Separate the stolen identity from the permissions it exercised, and test revocation across sessions.
- Test the recovery journey: multiple accounts, obsolete contact channels and accessible support.
- Use synthetic records and authorized staff, never real victims' information, for control tests.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred investigative guidance drawn from the sources.
Notes: The no-exploit-recipe limit and trust-boundary focus from [1]; corporate-boundary and customer-recovery verification priorities from the study's section 13.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What the retelling overstates and understates

Left: Easy to overstate
- A reset request to all users read as a confirmed count of unique victims.
- Encrypted passwords read as a guarantee that no password is recoverable.
- A separate-network statement read as permanent isolation of every brand.
Right: Easy to understate
- Non-financial profile data that persists and can enable impersonation.
- The corporate access boundary that preceded the customer reset.
- Reset completion that depends entirely on a stated denominator.
Caption: Evidence grade: editorial corrections the case requires.
Notes: Overstatement cautions from [1], [2] and [5]; the persistence and access-boundary points from the study's sections 5 and 6; keep claimed versus confirmed distinct.

## CLOSE | Precision beats a reset headline

- A stolen employee credential becomes a breach only where surrounding access permits it.
- Keep breach window, discovery and disclosure dates distinct in any timeline.
- Preserve the noun on every number: records, accounts, active buyers and people differ.
- Open gaps: no credential-acquisition method, no named actor, no invented victim or fraud total.
Caption: Evidence grade: case-specific conclusions, scope bounded.
Notes: Conclusion synthesis across the study; rests on [1] through [7]; protecting credentials, containing access and restoring confidence are related but none substitutes for the others.
