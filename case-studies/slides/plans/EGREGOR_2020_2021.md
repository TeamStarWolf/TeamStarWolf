## PART 1 | A malware name is not an organization chart

- A name may identify a code family, a service, an extortion brand or a set of people; a report should say which it means.
- ANSSI distinguishes operators from developers and treats Sekhmet and Maze links as similarity-based assessments. [1]
- Similar code can support shared development; similar delivery can support operator continuity; a shared name alone supports neither.
- A defender can remediate an observed access path while leaving the exact business relationship between criminals unresolved.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Early-stage compromise deserves its own closure criteria

- A blocked attachment is a narrower event than a confirmed implant running on an endpoint; keep that distinction visible.
- The absence of a final encryption event can reflect prevention, interruption, delay or incomplete visibility, each needing different evidence.
- Closure criteria should address the observed access, since credentials or sessions can persist after the executable is removed.
- A useful handover carries delivery evidence, execution sequence, affected identity and logging limits, not just "possible ransomware".
Caption: Evidence grade: original defensive analysis, framed by the delivery research.
Notes: Closure-criteria and handover model are the study's original analysis, informed by [3] and [4]; do not invent prevented-ransomware counts.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Data theft and encryption are different work streams

- Encryption is an immediate availability problem; data theft is a confidentiality problem that can persist after systems operate normally.
- The confidentiality stream should distinguish access, collection, transfer and disclosure; a staged archive is not proof of exfiltration.
- A recovered application may still depend on credentials or interfaces under investigation, so restoration must not reintroduce unsafe access.
- A status update can say a service resumed while exposure assessment continues; that is accurate, not contradictory.
Caption: Evidence grade: original defensive analysis, no victim inventory asserted.
Notes: Two-work-stream model and the access-versus-exfiltration distinction are the study's original analysis; a file count is not a count of people affected.

## CLOSE | Preserve both the evidence and its date

- Respond to the access actually observed, and distinguish a reported victim count from a verified census.
- Treat disruption as a scoped action, not a guarantee that every victim was restored.
- Preserve recovery artifacts deliberately, since a note lost as disposable messaging can forfeit a later option.
- Validate a new decryptor on copies before claiming success, and keep confidentiality assessment independent of recovery.
