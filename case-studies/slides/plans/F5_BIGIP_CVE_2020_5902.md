## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | A management-plane failure, not a victim census

- CVE-2020-5902 exposed a consequential weakness in BIG-IP's administrative interface, TMUI.
- A load balancer's management service is a different security surface from the applications it delivers.
- Disclosure and corrective software preceded an early-July burst of scanning, exploit material and hostile activity.
- The record supports serious consequences without an invented victim count, loss total or single attribution.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed vulnerability; no incident attributed in the record.
Notes: Executive-assessment framing from [1] and [2]; no exploit requests or payloads reproduced.

## PART 1 | Keep vulnerability, exploit and incident distinct

- A severity label describes potential, not a demonstrated or in-the-wild outcome.
- Keep exploitable-in-theory separate from exploited-in-the-wild, and both from a confirmed incident.
- Product capability over an appliance is not proof of observed theft on any one device.
- Each timeline entry should name both its event and its evidence: vendor, researcher, sensor or national alert.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed definitions; no universal first-exploitation time.
Notes: Evidence-level discipline from the executive assessment and [1]; timeline-record rule from section 1.

## PART 1 | The administrative surface was the relevant target

- F5's page states remote access to TMUI enabled the behavior and warns an exposed, uncorrected unit may already be compromised. [1]
- The public service address a customer uses and the operator's administrative path can carry different controls.
- An exposure review that inventories only branded hostnames can miss the interface that actually mattered.
- "Company uses BIG-IP" does not establish that its TMUI was internet reachable.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (F5 response page); impact stays conditional.
Notes: TMUI scope and the "may already be compromised" warning from [1].

## PART 1 | How the request-processing boundary failed

- NCC Group's root-cause analysis describes Apache httpd forwarding selected requests to Tomcat via mod_proxy_ajp. [8]
- The resource admitted by one layer could differ from the resource interpreted downstream.
- Calling it only "directory traversal" is too shallow; the question is whether authorization stays attached to the final resource.
- No operational requests, payloads or bypass syntax are reproduced.
Caption: Evidence grade: Demonstrated root cause (NCC Group, July 12).
Notes: Front-end-to-downstream interpretation gap from [8]; no exploit recipe included.

## PART 2 | The July 7-8 mitigation reversal

- Canada's Cyber Centre updated its alert July 9, describing F5's July 8 revision as addressing a mitigation bypass. [4]
- Keep "mitigated" distinct from "patched": a temporary request filter is not the software correction.
- Authenticated TMUI users could still exploit the flaw until patching, an explicit residual condition.
- Reducing public reachability lowered exposure but remained an incomplete substitute for corrected software.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (Cyber Centre AL20-018, updated July 9).
Notes: July 8 revision, the bypass and authenticated residual risk from [4].

## PART 2 | The first week held more than scanning

- SANS ISC's July 5 diary records scans and a redacted request carrying a Nuclei user-agent; the request is omitted. [10]
- A scan shows interest in a route; it does not prove the request succeeded or name the sender.
- NCC Group's intelligence post records staged payloads and web shells during July 6-8. [3]
- Once an actor changes appliance state, fixing the entry condition does not necessarily remove those changes.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed observation [10]; Reported chronology [3].
Notes: Scan limits from [10]; staged activity and the change-log caveat from [3].

## PART 2 | Management ports, Self IPs and reused certificates

Left: Coverage was not one route
- TMUI could be reachable via the management port and via configured Self IP addresses. [11]
- "Management access restricted" may cover only one address or interface, not the whole service.
Right: Recovery could reach past the appliance
- CERT-FR advises certificate revocation and replacement when unauthorized access is suspected. [11]
- It includes other equipment sharing the same wildcard certificates, without proving universal theft.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed guidance (CERT-FR ALE-015).
Notes: Management and Self IP distinction and the conditional certificate guidance from [11].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Confirmed incidents outrank scans and tool output

- CISA's July 24 alert reports two confirmed compromises while other investigations continued. [2]
- The number two is bounded by one responder's visibility, not generalized up or down to a global total.
- F5's indicator tool runs on the device it assesses, so a negative report is not an independent attestation. [12]
- Log rotation or adversarial cleanup can remove evidence; a positive indicator triggers interpretation, not attribution.

## CLOSE | One filter question was never enough

- The response failed if it asked only whether a request filter had been installed.
- The record required separate propositions: which management paths were reachable, which boundary was vulnerable.
- Then: what the mitigation covered, whether corrected software was active, what evidence existed of earlier intrusion.
- Each proposition needs its own evidence; where it is missing, the uncertainty stays visible.
