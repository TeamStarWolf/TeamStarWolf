## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | More than an account-takeover campaign

- The population visible in an early public leak may not represent everything an intruder obtained.
- This was more than account takeover: trial evidence describes a compromised employee computer and a stolen login database.
- Strong member behavior cannot replace the provider's duty to protect the store and the access paths that reach it.
- Company, civil and criminal records are not interchangeable; their financial figures should not be combined.

## PART 1 | Initial confirmation and the known collection

- June 6, 2012: LinkedIn confirmed that some compromised passwords matched member accounts and began staged resets.
- June 12: it reported theft of approximately 6.5 million passwords, with no corresponding email logins in the list.
- The first reset email contained no links; a reset link followed only after a user request.
- It said the transition to salted hashing was complete for the live store, which cannot protect a copy already taken.

## PART 1 | A published list is not the maximum taken

- The public appearance of one dataset proves that dataset exists, not the maximum data removed.
- A targeted reset is proportionate when the affected population is well established, less so when the list is only the sample.
- A provider should document why the chosen boundary is credible and what evidence would widen it.
- Keep directly confirmed records distinct from the broader systems and time periods potentially exposed.

## PART 2 | The renewed 2016 response to historical data

- May 17, 2016: LinkedIn learned 2012 data was being made available online and called this historical exposure, not a new breach.
- May 18: Cory Scott described a claimed collection of more than 100 million member email-and-hash pairs.
- May 25: the preserved notice listed email addresses, hashed passwords and internal member IDs.
- It invalidated passwords for accounts created before 2012 that had not reset since the incident.

## PART 3 | Employee endpoint to the password database

- Trial evidence described compromise of a LinkedIn employee computer and malware allowing remote control.
- The offender used employee credentials to reach the corporate VPN, then stole a database of user login information.
- Successful VPN authentication should not by itself authorize bulk export of the member-password store.
- The record does not detail every permission or detection gap; no exploit chain is reconstructed here.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed via the sentencing account of trial evidence.

## PART 4 | Password storage as a provider duty

- Storage has two jobs: prevent access to the store, and limit the damage if a copy is taken.
- A salt makes identical chosen passwords store differently; computational work raises the cost of testing a guess.
- A completed live-store upgrade cannot retroactively protect a copy already removed in 2012.
- A migration needs a measured population; dormant accounts can linger on an old representation.

## CIVIL & CRIMINAL | Separate figures, separate proceedings

Left: Civil settlement (2015)
- A $1.25 million fund for a defined class of US premium subscribers, March 2006 through June 2012.
- An overpayment-for-security theory, not a finding that each member suffered identity theft.
- Included a five-year password-protection obligation.
Right: Criminal case
- An 88-month sentence for intrusions at LinkedIn, Dropbox and Formspring.
- A $1,734,000 multi-company restitution award was later rejected as insufficiently supported.
- These figures come from separate proceedings and must not be added into one loss total.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Verify the boundary around the store

- Verify the access boundary around the authentication store before treating corporate entry as license to retrieve it.
- Investigate endpoint, identity and database access together; a replaced credential beside a live implant invites re-theft.
- Give any reset an explicit denominator and a verifiable state change for the old secret.
- Keep known leaked subsets, suspected exposure and later confirmed records in separate fields.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | Overstated versus understated

Left: Easy to overstate
- A 2016 re-surfacing read as a fresh hack of the live platform.
- Combining the civil fund and criminal restitution into one breach-loss number.
- A headline count read as a tally of unique people who suffered fraud.
Right: Easy to understate
- Documented corporate access and theft of a login database, not isolated password guesses.
- Historical credentials staying usable for years if a service still accepts them.
- Credential reuse as the real cross-service risk after exposure.

## CLOSE | Closure cannot rest on the first public list

- Incident closure cannot depend on the first collection that becomes public.
- Historical authentication data can stay relevant for years and force a renewed response.
- Separate exposure, potential misuse and demonstrated harm; keep each figure tied to its proceeding.
- A durable program links employee-endpoint control, store protection and customer recovery with an evidence record.
