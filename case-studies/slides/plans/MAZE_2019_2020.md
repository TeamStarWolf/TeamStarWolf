## PART 1 | Keep claimed separate from confirmed

- Double extortion is an organizing description, not evidence that every organization suffered both outcomes.
- An attacker's assertion is a lead; it is not a verified victim census or data inventory.
- "Targeted" is not "encrypted"; "affected" is not "compromised"; each needs its own evidence.
- A ransom demand is a claim by operators, separate from any confirmed payment.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Three outcomes need separate acceptance

- A Maze-like incident spans unauthorized activity, service availability and information exposure.
- Each category needs its own evidence, completion criteria and named decision-maker.
- A status report can say encryption was prevented, an account is under investigation, and exposure assessment continues.
- That is more informative than a binary of safe or unsafe, and it justifies continued response resources.
Caption: Evidence grade: Original defensive analysis.
Notes: Three-category model and named acceptance owners from the study's analysis sections.

## DEFENDER VIEW | A blocked attempt is still an incident

- An encryption alert marks a visible action, not necessarily the start of compromise; a backward review is warranted.
- Preserve the gap between the first observed event and the earliest possible event; name the retention boundary.
- A compromised privileged account is a recovery dependency; resetting one password is rarely sufficient.
- After a blocked attempt, assume the actor may try again; confirm access, persistence and control paths are addressed.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred guidance drawn from the reported cases.
Notes: Backward review, privileged-access dependency and repeated attempts from the analysis; echoes [2][3].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Blocking an application is narrower than blocking an outcome

- An application restriction may stop one client while leaving another authorized interface to the same service.
- Define the data movement to govern; test approved channels with harmless data, never a real criminal upload.
- A virtual machine changes visibility, not responsibility; a guest can reach host or network resources.
- Evidence of a transfer tool is not proof of a completed transfer; leave unresolved uncertainty visible.
Caption: Evidence grade: Original analysis built on the reported application and VM techniques.
Notes: Control-scope, VM visibility and transfer-evidence points from the analysis; grounded in [2][3].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Provider disruption, and confidentiality after restoration

- A provider's internal systems can be client dependencies, so impact can exceed any encrypted system.
- A reconnection package should address the specific access path, containment, evidence limits and monitoring.
- Restoration removes unavailability pressure but cannot reverse a completed transfer of information.
- Separate access, staging, transfer and disclosure; an attacker claim is a lead, not a final inventory.
Caption: Evidence grade: Original dependency and confidentiality analysis.
Notes: Service-provider mapping and exposure investigation from the analysis; disruption context from [6][7][8].

## CLOSE | Explain what was stopped and what remains to establish

- Technical success at one stage is not resolution of the whole incident.
- An ecosystem report, an unnamed response case and a company disclosure are different kinds of evidence.
- Make completion criteria explicit: access ended, function restored, exposure scoped, reconnection evidenced.
- The case justifies rigorous scoping and coordinated recovery, not a universal access story or a product guarantee.
