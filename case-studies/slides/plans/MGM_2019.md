## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | The 2019 breach, not the 2023 attack

- MGM's 2019 guest-data breach is a separate incident from its September 2023 cyberattack.
- The most revealing feature is the difference between stopping an intrusion and completing a response.
- A technically contained incident can still leave population analysis, notification and accountability open.
- The study separates three evidence kinds: MGM's technical account, the Canadian regulator's findings and US litigation.

## PART 2 | Developer access was several boundaries, not one

- A developer identity is one entry point; authentication enforcement is another; a token is a further relationship to data.
- Reused employee credentials opened a developer platform and a coding flaw bypassed multifactor authentication.
- Describing this only as a cloud breach loses the distinct boundaries that actually failed.
- Original logs and third-party code were not available; the path is MGM's account, not a reproduced exploit.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported technical account [1]; dependency analysis is Inferred.
Notes: Boundary distinctions and the dependency problem from [1]; no vendor, token value or bypass procedure is invented.

## PART 3 | Short access can have long consequences

- An access duration is not a harm duration; a token can expire while information copied through it stays usable.
- The useful questions: what the token could reach, how much it retrieved, whether access was observable, how to revoke it.
- Rotating a secret may block another request; it does not change a copied passport number or birth date.
- Keep separate completion states: access terminated, data scope established, populations assessed, notifications done.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred analysis anchored to the documented path [1].
Notes: Access-versus-harm framing from [1]; these are analytical questions, not proven missing safeguards.

## PART 4 | Buying a copy is not recovering exclusivity

- The chronology includes buying a copy from the attacker, not demonstrated recovery of every copy.
- A digital data set is replicable; a removed listing says little about copies held by sellers or intermediaries.
- The data resurfaced for sale in February 2020.
- "Recovered" implies more than the evidence supports; state the action taken and its verified result.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported purchase and resale [1]; the confidentiality caution is Inferred.
Notes: Purchase on July 24, 2019 after law-enforcement consultation, and February 2020 resale, from [1]; claimed recovery stays distinct from confirmed recovery.

## PART 5 | Data quality must be tested, not assumed

- OPC analysts found the supposedly disorganized data reasonably usable.
- A claim that information is hard to interpret is a proposition about an adversary's effort, not a control.
- Disorganization, encryption and deletion are different states and should not be treated as equivalent.
- Partial expiry does not settle risk: an invalid identification document still links to a stable name or birth date.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed OPC finding on usability [1]; the testability framing is Inferred.
Notes: Usability finding and resale from [1]; a field-by-field and combined-information assessment beats one reassuring adjective.

## PART 6 | A customer relationship can cross borders

- A customer relationship can cross borders even when the stay and the affected infrastructure sit in one country.
- Finishing a familiar domestic workflow first can delay the moment an applicable reporting question is asked.
- Create a population-and-obligation workstream early so legal analysis is not excluded by an engineering team's scope.
- The OPC finding is a specific reporting and notification outcome, not a universal verdict on every control.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed OPC outcome [1]; the sequencing recommendation is Inferred.
Notes: Cross-border response framing from [1]; the finding should not become a claim that every affected person suffered identity theft.

## PART 7 | A pleading ruling is not a trial verdict

- On November 2, 2022 the District of Nevada granted MGM's dismissal motion in part and denied it in part.
- Dismissed without leave to amend: negligence on lost time alone, negligent misrepresentation and certain statutory claims.
- Unjust enrichment was dismissed with leave to amend.
- A pleading-stage ruling sorts which theories proceed; it does not authenticate allegations or resolve the merits.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed court order [2]; a pleading-stage disposition, not a verdict.
Notes: Disposition from [2], Case 2:20-cv-00376, Document 128; the litigation and the OPC finding involve different questions and standards.

- A subgroup with government identifiers is not a second population to add to all affected residents.
- A class covering two incidents is not automatically the number affected by the 2019 incident alone.
- Keep records exposed or at risk separate from records confirmed accessed or misused.

## CLOSE | A bounded account beats a borrowed 2023 story

- Do not assign 2023 actor names, ransomware traits or hotel-system disruption to the 2019 breach.
- Do not call the joint fund a 2019-only fine, or a temporary access period a long-term dwell estimate.
- Confidentiality loss and incident governance can continue long after an access path is closed.
- The case shows where to ask precise questions: developer-access boundaries, usable data and cross-border notice.
