## OVERVIEW | Why macro-only framing fell short
- CVE-2021-40444 was a Windows MSHTML remote-code-execution flaw reached through malicious Office documents in 2021.
- The vulnerable component sat in Windows, so the risk extended past whether a user had enabled VBA macros.
- The public response moved through an advisory, interim restrictions, and the September 14 updates, which were not equivalent stages.
- A later bypass claim was overstated, and the corrected account limits the attack to unpatched systems.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (CVE record and dated CERT notices)
Notes: This slide states the thesis that component reach and fix sequencing carry the case, not macro status alone.

## BOUNDARY | Delivery surface versus execution
Left: Delivery surface
- Opening a document is a user action at a trust boundary.
- The document could invoke the Internet Explorer rendering engine to process a remote page.
- ActiveX was involved in the observed malware delivery path.
Right: Execution reality
- A feature reaching another component is not itself a vulnerability.
- The MSHTML flaw let that component do something unintended.
- Describing only the attachment hides the Windows component boundary.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (CERT-EU, Microsoft CVE record)
Notes: This slide keeps document delivery and open distinct from the automatic execution that followed.

## MECHANISM | How the document reached MSHTML
- The Microsoft-authored CVE record names malicious Office documents and a malicious ActiveX control as the route to the MSHTML flaw.
- MSHTML, the Trident rendering engine, is a Windows component, not an Office-only feature.
- Consequences depended on privileges, with lower-rights users less affected than administrators.
- The CVE database publication date of September 15 is not the September 7 advisory date.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (Microsoft-authored CVE record)
Notes: This slide traces the Office-to-component route without reproducing any exploit detail.

## CONTROLS | Macros and isolation answered different questions
Left: Macro question
- Traditional VBA macros were not necessary for the route.
- Absence of a macro did not establish absence of an execution path.
- An investigation cannot stop at asking whether the file was macro-enabled.
Right: Component reach
- Research discussed preview and RTF-related routes.
- A control can block one scripting feature and leave another component reachable.
- Described variants were interim assessment, not all confirmed production intrusions.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (Huntress contemporary analysis)
Notes: This slide distinguishes a macro check from the broader question of reachable components.

## CONTEXT | Protected View and Mark-of-the-Web mattered
- Protected View or Application Guard provided default protection for internet-origin documents.
- The Canadian alert described a user enabling editing in the path it summarized.
- Microsoft observed immediate payload execution when a document lacked the mark identifying internet origin.
- The relevant state includes origin marking and the processing mode, not a universal prompt sequence.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (Canadian Cyber Centre, Microsoft)
Notes: This slide records that the internet-origin mark and protection mode changed the outcome.

## MITIGATION | Interim controls were not patch equivalence
Left: The interim controls
- JPCERT recorded the initial workaround restricting ActiveX control installation.
- Later guidance covered Group Policy and disabling Explorer file preview.
- CERT-EU separated new-installation restrictions from controls already installed.
Right: What each changes
- A content restriction affects permitted behavior.
- A software correction changes vulnerable behavior.
- The three defenses do not produce identical evidence of protection.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (JPCERT, CERT-EU)
Notes: This slide keeps the ActiveX-disable workaround separate from the September correction.

## CLOSURE | Patch availability versus advisory closure
- The September 14 update is confirmed by the Microsoft CVE record and multiple dated CERT records.
- CERT-FR's September 15 revision recommended the patch and, where applicable, removing the preceding workaround.
- Closing the alert on May 4, 2022 did not mean the threat had ended.
- Neither a page's open status nor its closure date establishes the patch state of a particular computer.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (Microsoft CVE record, CERT-FR)
Notes: This slide separates administrative closure of an advisory from the technical patch state of an endpoint.

## CORRECTION | The CAB-less claim was walked back
- Sophos described a short October 24-25 spam campaign using a changed delivery format and Formbook malware.
- The original presentation implied the new method could bypass the September patch.
- Two December 23 corrections state that implication was wrong, with later attack behavior blocked on a patched test system.
- End-to-end retesting was limited because the remote malicious site was no longer available.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (Sophos, corrected 23 December)
Notes: This slide treats the correction as evidence, since a connection did not prove the full exploit still worked.

## CORROBORATION | Later research around an effective fix
- Elastic's January 24, 2022 account acknowledged the adjacent Sophos work and overlapping campaign research.
- It described infrastructure linking testing and production phases and said patching appeared effective.
- The campaign moved toward a traditional malware-attachment phishing approach.
- Continued malicious email does not by itself show continued success through the original vulnerability.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (Elastic security research)
Notes: This slide reads later vendor work as adaptation around a working fix, not a separate victim population.

## FINDINGS | What the record supports
- The evidence supports a document-driven route to a Windows component flaw and targeted exploitation before the patch.
- It supports a sequence of evolving interim restrictions, a September 14 correction, and later attacker experimentation.
- It does not support a single worldwide loss figure, a complete victim count, or one named operator for all campaigns.
- A document assessment must follow invoked components, and a patch assessment must follow the result of execution.
