- Subtitle: Two linked federal breaches show why record counts, attribution and containment each demand evidence, not headline compression.
- Published: 15 November 2026
- Notes: Evidence-bounded account; agency notification, congressional oversight, independent audit and a dated public attribution, each used within its limits.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Record count alone understates what was lost

- **Vetting data** combines identity, relationships and personal history, so the risk differs from a contact-list leak.
- Defenders must treat sensitive data as an asset and separate removing one foothold from full containment.
- A successful action against an observed intruder does not prove every access path is gone.
- A control called implemented may still carry exceptions, missing coverage or ineffective operation.
- Recovery has limits: securing a network cannot retrieve copies of already stolen personal history.
Caption: Evidence grade: agency notification and oversight findings, stated within limits.
Notes: From the Executive assessment; grounded in the OPM FAQ [1] and later audits [6][7].

## KEY FACTS | Populations overlap; the total is 22.1M, not 25.7M

Stat: 4.2M | Personnel incident, current and former staff
Stat: 21.5M | Background-investigation individuals
Stat: 3.6M | Overlap counted once
Stat: 22.1M | Deduplicated affected population
- The background population: 19.7M applicants and 1.8M non-applicants, mainly spouses or cohabitants.
- 4.2 + 21.5 - 3.6 = 22.1M; summing the figures to 25.7M double counts the 3.6M overlap.
- A person is not a record, form or field, and one form can mention several people.
- Categories were defined for the agency's assessment and notification, not as an exact census.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, the contemporaneous OPM FAQ.
Notes: The OPM FAQ preserved by the National Archives [1].

## PART 1 | The fingerprint subset grew to 5.6M, not a new group

- Early material cited about 1.1M records with fingerprints; the archived figure is about 5.6M.
- Use 5.6M for established scope; keep 1.1M only in a dated chronology of what was then known.
- The 5.6M is records within the background incident, not 5.6M new people or counted fingers.
- Biometric data is permanent, but exposure is not proof that any biometric system was bypassed.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed exposure; impersonation not demonstrated.
Notes: The early FAQ [1] and the revised OPM resource page [2].

## PART 1 | Sensitive content is not access to every system

- Background records held identity, residence, education, employment, relationships and some history.
- OPM reported no evidence that separate health, payroll, financial or retirement systems were hit.
- Do not label every affected person a top-secret clearance holder; use the source's broader terms.
- Ordinary fraud monitoring covers some financial risk and leaves other consequences out of scope.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported by OPM for that incident.
Notes: The OPM announcement republished by the US Army [3].

## PART 2 | A second actor survived the first remediation

- The House 2016 staff report describes an observed actor X1 and a second intrusion X2 using contractor credentials.
- Chronology: X2 access May 2014; background exfiltration Jul to Aug; personnel in December; fingerprints early 2015.
- X2 persisted beyond remediation of the observed actor, exposing incomplete authentication and visibility.
- X1 and X2 are analytical labels in a reconstruction, not proof of two named individuals or full attribution.
- The report keeps explicit uncertainty about exact entry and persistence details.
Caption: Evidence grade: a congressional reconstruction, uncertainty retained.
Notes: The House Oversight majority staff report [4].

## PART 2 | Attribution is one dated official statement

- In a 7 July 2020 speech, FBI Director Wray publicly attributed the OPM theft to China's hackers.
- He framed large stolen datasets as useful for identifying people with sensitive access.
- The speech distinguished the Chinese government and party from Chinese people and Chinese Americans.
- This supports a dated state attribution, not a named unit, every operator or every downstream use.
- Date the statement and its speaker; do not read it as a criminal verdict against an individual.
Caption: Evidence grade: a dated official attribution, 2020.
Notes: FBI Director Wray, Hudson Institute speech [5].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Completed work is not the same as effective work

Audits measured a defined recommendation set, and a completion claim is not the same as effectiveness.

| Review | What it found |
|---|---|
| GAO 2017, 19 US-CERT recs | 11 complete; 4 complete but still need work; 4 in progress |
| GAO 2018, 80 recs | 51 implemented; 29 open at 20 Sep 2018 |
| The 29 open | 5 insufficient evidence; 24 no evidence supplied |
| Caveat | "No evidence" is not "no work"; both figures are dated |
Caption: Evidence grade: dated audit findings, not a current scorecard.
Notes: GAO-17-614 [6] and GAO-19-143R [7].

## DEFENDER VIEW | One login should not grant broad internal reach

- Model the boundaries: external or contractor identity, access gateway, admin environment, repository, exit paths.
- If every login grants broad internal reach, one identity compromise becomes a large data compromise.
- A contractor may process a case without unrestricted access to unrelated cases or bulk exports.
- Separate ordinary work from privileged administration; privilege should be identifiable and reviewable.
- Test boundaries with authorized workflows and synthetic records; a segmented diagram alone is not proof.
Caption: Original defensive analysis, not a description of current OPM infrastructure.
Notes: Original analysis; builds on the contractor-credential intrusion [4].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Enforce authentication by path, then prove containment

- Strong-credential enrollment totals do not show where strong authentication is actually enforced.
- Inventory contractor access, remote administration, recovery and exception paths with owners and evidence.
- Authentication does not settle authorization; a valid account can still hold excessive access.
- Define contained: a disabled account, an isolated host and all identified paths are different claims.
- Keep a coverage record; a missing log is not proof that no activity occurred.
Caption: Original defensive validation questions, for authorized environments only.
Notes: Original analysis; the containment lesson grounded in [4].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Protect the whole data lifecycle

- Sensitive data lives in production, working extracts, backups and archives; know which copies are needed.
- A restricted primary database can be undermined by a broad export or an ungoverned recovery copy.
- Evaluate encryption in context: which identities can obtain readable output, and who controls keys.
- Test restoration as security; a backup restored to a weaker environment can create new exposure.
- Some details, such as history and biometrics, cannot be reset, so support must outlast one password change.
Caption: Evidence grade: original analysis; data categories per OPM.
Notes: Original analysis; data categories [3], biometric permanence [2].

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What the record overstates and understates

Left: Overstated when compressed
- A single 25.7M headline that adds overlapping populations.
- A top-secret clearance holder behind every affected person.
- A breach of every federal health or payroll system.
- A named-group attribution pulled from a malware label.
Right: Understated when minimized
- Vetting data's blend of identity, relationships and history.
- Non-applicants and contacts named on other people's forms.
- Biometric permanence that outlasts a password reset.
- A second actor that persisted past the first cleanup.
Caption: The same record misleads if compressed and if minimized.
Notes: FAQ [1], resource page [2], announcement [3], House report [4].

## CLOSE | Evidence, not headlines, closes a breach

- Constrain identity-based access so one compromise does not become total compromise.
- Validate containment beyond the first observed foothold.
- Require evidence before marking a corrective action closed.
- Stewardship outlasts the incident; securing a system does not undo disclosed personal history.
Caption: A credible response keeps technical rigor and honesty about recovery's limits.
Notes: Conclusion; synthesizes the study and sources [1]-[7].
