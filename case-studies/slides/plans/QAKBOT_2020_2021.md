# Slide plan: QakBot 2020-2021

- Study: case-studies/QAKBOT_2020_2021.md
- Cover title: Stolen conversations / Loader economics / Bounded evidence
- Subtitle: A dated, source-bounded account of QakBot delivery, email collection and modular access across 2020 and 2021, from vendor and advisory reporting.
- Published: 18 November 2026
- Notes: The cover frames QakBot as a long-lived loader whose most consequential asset was stolen correspondence, read strictly against dated primary sources.

## ASSESSMENT | A banking trojan that also brokered access
- QakBot in 2020-2021 is a sequence of documented campaigns, not one breach with one victim.
- The same operation collected correspondence, maintained access and enabled later attacks.
- A QakBot detection does not by itself identify a ransomware operator.
- Removing the implant does not prove stolen messages or secondary footholds are neutralized.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (primary vendor investigations and a joint CISA/FBI advisory)
Notes: The study treats malware, distributors, infrastructure and follow-on attackers as related but distinct subjects.

## METHOD | Four kinds of evidence, kept separate
The case distinguishes four evidence types that reinforce one another but must not substitute for one another.

| Evidence type | What it can establish |
| --- | --- |
| Captured artifact | What an examined sample contains |
| Researcher observation | Behavior under the reported conditions |
| Response telemetry | Behavior connected to an affected environment |
| Intelligence assessment | Proposed relationships among incidents |
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred (evidence taxonomy applied across the sources)
Notes: None of the reviewed sources is a census of every QakBot infection, and selected reading scopes are stated rather than implied.

## JUNE 2020 | The captured document, the missing email
- FortiGuard Labs captured a malicious Word document on 11 June 2020 but did not know how it was delivered.
- The macro sequence obtained an executable disguised by a PNG extension, with several candidate download locations.
- The examined payload used packing and checks associated with analysis environments.
- "Probably phishing" is a hypothesis; a recovered phishing message would be a different evidentiary claim.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed for the sample; delivery path Inferred
Notes: A researcher can understand a document fully without possessing the email that carried it.

## JULY 2020 | Collection and persistence in one variant
Stat: 150 | C2 address-and-port pairs in the config
Stat: 1 | core module running inside explorer.exe
- FortiGuard's 8 July follow-up demonstrated capture of clipboard content and keystrokes and examined browser interception.
- Persistence ran through registry and scheduled-task mechanisms, with local-network propagation.
- An observed capability and a measured loss against a specific victim remain separate claims.
Caption: Evidence grade: Demonstrated (controlled researcher observation of one variant)
Notes: The 150 pairs were a configuration finding, not a global count of active servers or infected organizations.

## COUNTING | Why a config list is not a server census
- A list of candidate destinations is not 150 functioning controllers at the moment of an intrusion.
- Some entries could be unavailable, reused or relevant only under particular conditions.
- The specimen held alternatives rather than one hard-coded point of dependence.
- A clean audit would separate embedded candidates, observed connections and confirmed infrastructure roles.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred (original analysis of reported configuration evidence)
Notes: Collapsing distinct columns into a single server count would manufacture false precision.

## CASEWORK | Personal email as entry, work mail as loss
- Kroll traced a compromise to interaction with a malicious link in an employee's personal email on a work computer.
- The malware collected thousands of messages and contacts across multiple users.
- Broader cases included locally stored messages more than three years old.
- Observed cases provided no evidence that attachments were included in the collected data.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (response telemetry from investigated environments)
Notes: The date on a stolen message is not necessarily the intrusion date.

## DISTRIBUTION | Emotet volumes are not QakBot infections
Stat: 160 | days Emotet absent from observed delivery
Stat: ~250K | Emotet messages seen on the return day
Stat: 7M+ | summer Emotet messages, about 40 days
Stat: 6M+ | Jan-Feb Emotet messages, about 20 days
- Proofpoint reported Emotet's 17 July return and a 28 August change to a new Qbot affiliate.
- A delivered message can be blocked, ignored, opened without execution or followed by a failed download.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (vendor distribution telemetry)
Notes: These totals describe the observed Emotet mail stream and do not belong on a chart titled "QakBot victims."

## DELIVERY | Lures and containers changed through 2020
Dated vendor reporting records distinct delivery changes within each source's visibility.

| Date | Source | Observed delivery change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 Jun 2020 | FortiGuard | Word macro fetching a PNG-named executable |
| 27 Aug 2020 | Check Point | VBS delivery, padding, server-side module choices |
| 15 Oct 2020 | Hornetsecurity | XLSB workbook via hijacked conversation |
| Nov 2020 | Malwarebytes | Election-themed ZIP on hijacked reply threads |
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (each change bounded to the source's visibility)
Notes: A compilation timestamp can be manipulated and does not establish a first real-world infection date.

## PRETEXT | The DocuSign lure was not a DocuSign breach
Left: The crafted lure
- The workbook falsely presented itself as DocuSign-protected at roughly 12:40 UTC on 15 October 2020.
- Excel 4.0 macros obtained the loader after the user enabled content.
- Borrowed conversation context and a familiar brand supported the trust decision.
Right: What it was not
- The claim was a lure, not proof the service was breached or had encrypted the file.
- The prompt reframes enabling content as the step that makes a document readable.
- It is actually the step that permits additional behavior.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed sample; lure reading Inferred
Notes: The case is not adequately explained by saying "the user clicked."

## LOOP | Stolen conversation becomes the next lure
- Correspondence is not only a source of secrets; it is material for later impersonation.
- The model separates acquisition, construction of a reply, delivery and any resulting infection.
- A convincing reply does not prove the apparent sender's account was controlled at that moment.
- An organization can supply credible context used against a correspondent without knowingly sending the later message.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred (original analytical model over the reviewed cases)
Notes: Message authenticity, sender-account state and endpoint state remain different investigative questions.

## 2021 | Modules broadened the post-infection choices
- Trend Micro described a shift from embedded components to components downloaded after C2 contact.
- The inventory included credential and cookie collection, email harvesting, proxy functions and lateral movement.
- It also listed hidden remote desktop access, Cobalt Strike and an Atera remote-management component found in October 2021.
- These were observed or assessed capabilities, not proof every infection used the full inventory.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (capability inventory from vendor analysis)
Notes: "Loader" should not be read as harmless, since access that admits later tools can be consequential before ransomware appears.

## GEOGRAPHY | Telemetry, not a global census
Left: Trend Micro's window
- Its view covered 25 March to 25 October 2021.
- Prominent countries: United States, Japan, Germany.
- Prominent industries: telecommunications, technology, education.
Right: Why a map misleads
- A country chart looks more definitive than its underlying measurement.
- Detections are not unique organizations, and deployment affects visibility.
- Less visible countries are not shown to be unaffected.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported (bounded to a stated collection window)
Notes: The findings are used qualitatively, without inventing an infection rate or population denominator.

## SCOPING | Loader infection is not the ransomware incident
Left: What the reports place
- Primary reports place QakBot within a broader ecosystem of later access and ransomware.
- Microsoft's December 2021 report described modular infections and access sold for later attacks.
- A later ransomware event requires its own evidence about how access progressed.
Right: What still needs proof
- An organization can suffer meaningful compromise without a ransom note appearing.
- A host, a ransomware-hit organization and a stolen mailbox can be one incident, not three.
- The sources give no consistent denominator for an infection-to-ransomware rate.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred (scoping discipline over the primary reports)
Notes: Downstream ransomware associations are reported relationships, not proof every infection led to ransomware.

## 2023 | Disruption was not complete remediation
- The CISA/FBI advisory described a 25 August 2023 operation that took over the infrastructure and severed victim-to-C2 communications.
- It expressly warned the operation did not remediate other malware or ransomware already installed.
- Previously copied correspondence does not return to exclusive control when a bot connection is cut.
- A successful infrastructure action and an unfinished victim investigation can coexist.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed (joint CISA/FBI advisory, used as a bounded afterword)
Notes: Disrupting a broker is not eradicating the actors it served, which is the study's closing discipline.
