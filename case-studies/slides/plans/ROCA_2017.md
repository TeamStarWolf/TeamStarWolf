# Slide plan: ROCA, 2017: A Key-Generation Flaw and the Work of Rebuilding Trust

- Study: case-studies/ROCA_2017.md
- Cover title: ROCA, 2017 / A Key-Generation Flaw / The Work of Rebuilding Trust
- Subtitle: A key can stay inside protective hardware while its public value exposes a generation flaw, so remediation must follow the whole trust lifecycle.
- Published: 15 November 2026
- Notes: Evidence-bounded account; separates a generation flaw from an RSA break, and detection from abuse.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | ROCA is a generation flaw, not a break of RSA

A private key can stay inside hardware while its public counterpart reveals a weak generation method.

- The response cannot stop at checking whether a key was exported, or at patching software.
- This is a vulnerability and remediation study, not a proven mass identity-theft campaign.
- Detection of an affected key, recovery of a private key, and evidence of abuse stay separate.
- The study distinguishes CVE-2017-15361 from RSA itself.
Caption: Scope: the 2017 disclosure, CERT, a vendor example, Estonia's eID.
Notes: The executive assessment frames the trust lifecycle and the generation-flaw distinction. [1][2]

## PART 1 | The flaw let keys be recovered from public values

- Infineon's library generated structured RSA primes, weakening key generation itself.
- Private-key material could be recovered from affected public keys without the device.
- The researchers separate this from a faulty RNG and from a general break of RSA.
- Restricting a public directory does not change the math of keys already issued.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed disclosure; verified for selected keys.
Notes: Disclosure dates notification Feb 1 and public release Oct 16, 2017; difficulty does not rise uniformly with key length. [1]

## PART 1 | CERT scoped the flaw to generation, not every operation

- VU#307015 names the Infineon library 1.02.013 and CVE-2017-15361.
- The affected operation is RSA key generation; ECC is outside this flaw.
- RSA keys made by a sound method can still be used with the library.
- The better investigator question is where and how a given key was created.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, the CERT coordination record.
Notes: CERT lists updates, replacement or newly generated keys, and warns against relying on the current cost of attacking larger keys. [2]

## PART 1 | A positive scan is detection, not a recovered key

- The detector identifies the affected structure from public keys only.
- A discrete-logarithm detector became the Python default on October 30, 2017.
- Some language ports kept older methods with different false-positive behavior.
- A positive result is not impersonation, decryption or proof of abuse.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, the repository documentation.
Notes: Reports need the detector method and version, not just "ROCA scan"; a public-key-only review avoids collecting private material. [3]

## PART 2 | The YubiKey 4 impact was real but bounded

- Affected: certain on-device RSA generation for PIV and OpenPGP on YubiKey 4.
- Mitigation guidance names versions 4.2.6 through 4.3.4.
- Not affected: ECC PIV, FIDO U2F, OTP, OATH, YubiKey NEO and the U2F key.
- Version 4.3.5 and later ship a changed generation implementation.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, the vendor advisory YSA-2017-01.
Notes: Advisory dated Oct 16, 2017 with a June 6, 2017 remediation milestone; inventory capabilities and uses, not only product names. [4]

## PART 2 | Estonia suspended certificates as a precaution

- On Nov 2, 2017 Estonia planned to block certificates on 760,000 at-risk ID cards.
- Blocking took effect at 24:00 on November 3, into November 4.
- Cards stayed valid as physical identity documents, and renewal routes were offered.
- The state reported no known e-identity theft while raising its threat assessment.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported, a contemporaneous government statement.
Notes: Priority went to work depending on electronic use, including healthcare and justice; a precaution is not proof of prior abuse. [5]

## DEFENDER VIEW | The remediation unit is the key's lifecycle, not the device

- Identify generation origin: device, library or service, period and confidence.
- Map enrollment: every relying system that accepts the public key or certificate.
- Create a supported replacement; a new cert with the same weak key is not a rekey.
- Retire old trust where required, and verify the retirement.
Caption: Original lifecycle analysis derived from the case, not a shared process.
Notes: A device replacement closes no trust relationship automatically; closure is the credential and its dependencies. [2][4]

## DEFENDER VIEW | A firmware fix does not change an already generated key

Left: Platform track
- Device eligibility and supported updates.
- Replacement where devices need it.
- Verified state afterward.
Right: Credential track
- Affected keys and replacement issuance.
- Relying-party changes.
- Retirement of the old credential.
Caption: The tracks should meet at a closure review, not be reported apart.
Notes: A green hardware dashboard can hide a still-trusted old signing certificate; plan recovery before irreversible resets. [2]

## DEFENDER VIEW | Closure needs evidence, not shortcuts

| Closure question | Evidence to keep | Shortcut to reject |
|---|---|---|
| Old key affected? | Scoped public-key check | Product logo alone |
| Generation path sound? | Supported, verified method | Longer key, no analysis |
| New key issued? | Distinct public-key identity | Cert renewal with reuse |
| New credential accepted? | Authorized functional test | Device shipment |
| Old trust retired? | Relying-party status evidence | User assurance |
| Users can recover? | Tested recovery route | Destructive reset |
Caption: Preserve failed and unknown results as their own categories.
Notes: Keep public identifiers apart from private material; a high completion percentage can hide the hardest cases. [3]

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What gets overstated, and what gets missed

Left: Overstated
- "RSA is broken" or "every TPM failed".
- Every affected ID equals a stolen identity.
- A scan result proves criminal use.
Right: Understated
- Old public keys linger in allowlists and configs.
- Device completion is not trust retirement.
- A new certificate may reuse a weak key.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred emphasis, grounded in the cited records.
Notes: Keep the overstated-versus-understated split; a revoked certificate is not erased history. [1][6]

## CLOSE | Cryptographic trust has an inventory and a lifecycle

- Do not call ROCA a general defeat of RSA or a universal TPM failure.
- A positive fingerprint is not criminal use, and a firmware update is not a rekey.
- A new certificate is not always a new key, and a revoked certificate is not erased.
- Know where a key lives, how it was generated, what it authorizes and how to replace it.
Caption: Make the trust dependency visible before the next emergency.
Notes: Do not merge ROCA with later token flaws, or treat 2017 cost estimates as current prices. [1][2]
