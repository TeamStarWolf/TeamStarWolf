## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | One incident combined four kinds of harm

The Sony Pictures incident fused destruction, theft, public exposure and coercive pressure in one event.

- Destruction created an immediate continuity problem that restoring servers could address.
- Disclosure created a harm that restoring a server cannot undo.
- Threats against people and partners added a safety and decision dimension.
- The record supports strong claims about disruption and US attribution, not every detail.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Destruction and theft need separate workstreams

- A destructive event demands containment and a trusted recovery environment.
- A theft demands assessing what data was accessible, copied, retained or disclosed.
- One workstream cannot close the other; a backup restore does not retrieve leaked copies.
- Assign explicit owners for system trust, information impact and business continuity.
Caption: Evidence grade: original defensive analysis.
Notes: Original analysis on separate workstreams.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Restore business functions, not just machines

- Map how payroll, payments, schedules and financial close depend on systems and approvals.
- Define a minimum viable operating state and an acceptance test for each function.
- Sequence recovery by consequence and dependency, not by how visible a system is.
- Reconciliation of outage-period records is a recovery control, not a later clerical task.
Caption: Evidence grade: original defensive analysis.
Notes: Original analysis on function-level recovery.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Plan for losing the normal communication system

- Email, directories and contact lists may be untrusted when they are most needed.
- Keep an alternative communication plan with activation criteria and named owners.
- Give recognizable verification steps that never ask for passwords or sensitive records.
- Maintain controlled offline contacts and a closure plan to capture decisions later.
Caption: Evidence grade: original defensive analysis.
Notes: Original analysis on survivable communications.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Employees are affected people, and coercion is cross-functional

Left: People-centered response
- Coordinate privacy, HR, security and operations around real categories.
- Separate confirmed findings from potential exposure and precaution.
- Measure whether notices reach former staff and contractors.
Right: Coercion decisions
- Route threats to people or places to safety and law-enforcement experts.
- Give a decision group authority and separate facts from adversary claims.
- Do not amplify hostile messages or reproduce attacker contact details.

## CLOSE | Prepare trusted recovery paths, not a single tool

- Prepare trusted recovery paths, independent communications and clear decision authority.
- Treat reconciliation and employee support as recovery, not afterthoughts.
- Preserve attribution and procedural status without amplifying stolen information.
- Run a bounded 90-day exercise with synthetic data, named owners and retest dates.
