## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Boundaries that did not contain risk

- The 2021 breach is about boundaries that did not contain risk, from lab environments to retained customer data.
- The documented chain moved from an initially reached environment to additional systems and stored customer information.
- Even after access is contained, affected populations, notices, settlement funds and regulatory duties must be examined separately.
- The later record spans a company account, an FCC consent decree, appellate litigation and a state complaint, each a different kind of fact.

## PART 3 | What the technical record actually supports

- The FCC account describes initial access to a lab through telecom equipment by impersonating a legitimate connection.
- Password guessing, movement between environments, scanning and password spraying led to environments holding database backups.
- Backups change the model: protecting the live interface does not protect every copy of the same information.
- The record does not name a single exploit, a full credential list or a complete network diagram; the study does not invent them.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed FCC account; no exploit code reproduced.
Notes: Access sequence, backup repositories and exfiltration from [3]; the no-single-exploit limit is the study's discipline.

## PART 3 | The CEO account and what it did not disclose

- The August 27 post described entry into testing environments and movement to other servers through brute force and other methods.
- It said Mandiant supported the investigation and announced longer-term work with Mandiant and KPMG.
- It is a contemporary high-level account and a record of commitments, not the forensic report or proof of completed fixes.
- Announcing partnerships is not the same as publishing measurable outcomes.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed company statement; outcomes not demonstrated.
Notes: CEO description, the Mandiant and KPMG work, and the apology framing from [4].

## PART 4 | Washington's complaint shifts the analytical focus

- The January 2025 action alleges known weaknesses, weak implementation of security practices and misleading assurances contributed to the breach.
- The complaint identifies weaknesses in risk governance, configuration, authentication, asset management and monitoring.
- It alleges insufficient rate limiting of authentication attempts and poor handling of external-connection alerts.
- These are allegations, not a final merits judgment; some technical passages are substantially redacted and not reconstructed.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported allegations, not adjudicated findings.
Notes: State allegations from [6]; governance, authentication and monitoring detail from [7]; redactions left intact.

## PART 4 | Notification quality became its own problem

- The complaint alleges some customers whose SSNs were exposed got texts stressing that card data was safe, without clearly stating the identifier exposure.
- It challenges placing important details behind a link and the form of electronic notice.
- A true statement about one unaffected category can crowd out a more consequential affected category.
- The study identifies the contested design, not a claim that every customer misunderstood.
Caption: Evidence grade: Reported allegation about notice design.
Notes: Notice contrasts, the link placement and the electronic-notice challenge from [7].

## PART 5 | The settlement fund is not a per-person payment

Stat: $350M | Class settlement fund
Stat: $150M | Security spending, 2022-2023
Stat: 40+ | Related actions centralized
- The 8th Circuit's July 29, 2024 opinion describes a $350 million fund, separate security spending, identity services and varying compensation paths.
- It reversed the attorneys' fee award and remanded; the appeal concerned fees, not whether the breach happened.
- A fund is not an equal payment to every person; administration, eligibility and documented losses shape what a claimant received.
- A September 2026 company filing reports the settlement final, $350 million paid and $150 million spent on security, with no admission of liability.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed opinion and later company filing.
Notes: Fund, fee reversal and structure from [5]; final status and spending from [2]; averages not asserted.

## PART 5 | The FCC deal spans more than the 2021 breach

Stat: $15.75M | Civil penalty
Stat: $15.75M | Cybersecurity investment
Stat: 3 | Incident years: 2021, 2022, 2023
- The September 2024 FCC announcement sets a $15.75 million civil penalty and a separate $15.75 million cybersecurity investment commitment.
- It resolves investigations into multiple incidents from 2021, 2022 and 2023; the two amounts are not a $31.5 million fine for August 2021.
- The consent decree records disagreement over whether the earlier program violated standards, even as both sides agreed to resolve.
- Later obligations can respond to a pattern across events, not prove one missing control at the first 2021 step.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Defensive lessons tied to the documented path

- Judge environment boundaries by reachable assets and privileges, not by names like "lab" or "test."
- Treat copies of customer data, including backups, as part of the same protection problem as primary systems.
- Test authentication and monitoring together, seeking evidence that controls operated, not a count of policies.
- Define completion separately for containment, investigation, notification, remediation and legal resolution.
Caption: Evidence grade: Inferred guidance from the documented path.
Notes: Boundary, backup, authentication-with-monitoring and completion lessons tied to [3] and [7].
