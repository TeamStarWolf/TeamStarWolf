## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Two failures, not one: access and the handling of facts

The case holds two distinct failures. One was access to sensitive data through credentials and cloud storage. The other was how the organization handled the resulting facts.

- **Access path and narrative are separate failures:** containing one did not resolve the other.
- When one group controls investigation, payment, classification and what reaches leadership, drift goes unnoticed.
- A program label cannot settle the factual question of what conduct actually occurred.
- A deletion promise is evidence of a promise, not proof every copy disappeared.
Caption: The practical goal is a system where bad news moves accurately.

## PART 1 | Stolen credentials reached a key, then cloud data

Per DOJ's July 2022 agreed facts, intruders used stolen credentials to enter a private source-code repository, obtained an access key and copied data from Uber's storage.

- The 2016 repository was private; do not assert it was public by analogy to the 2014 breach.
- A credential granting access beyond the repository turned a dev incident into a customer-data incident.
- About 57M user records and about 600,000 driver's-license numbers were identified.
- Uber accepted responsibility for personnel concealing the incident from the FTC.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, from DOJ agreed facts.
Notes: The private-repository boundary matters. Sources [1][2].

## PART 1 | A pending FTC inquiry reset the response context

The FTC was already investigating Uber's privacy and security practices when the 2016 breach occurred. Discovery of the undisclosed incident led the agency to expand the settlement.

- Final terms barred misrepresentations and required a company-wide privacy program.
- Biennial independent assessments were required for twenty years.
- Reporting is tied to a defined covered incident, not a universal ten-day rule for all firms.
- Governance lesson: keep a live map of active obligations and connect new evidence to them.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, from the consent order C-4662.
Notes: The order's definitions and conditions govern. Sources [3][4].

## PART 1 | Company and executive faced separate proceedings

Uber's July 2022 resolution was a corporate non-prosecution agreement, not an acquittal. On 5 October 2022 a jury convicted Joseph Sullivan of obstruction and misprision of a felony.

- DOJ cited management change, disclosure, cooperation and investment behind the corporate NPA.
- The jury account described concealment, a $100,000 bitcoin payment and agreements with false statements.
- The May 2023 sentence was three years probation and a $50,000 fine, not a statutory maximum.
- A corporate resolution does not decide individual liability, and vice versa.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed, the verdict and imposed sentence.
Notes: Probation is not prison and not acquittal. Sources [1][5][6].

## PART 2 | Appeals affirmed the conviction through June 2026

The Ninth Circuit's 12 November 2025 amended opinion affirmed the convictions and denied rehearing en banc, addressing jury instructions, sufficiency and a hacker's plea agreement.

- Any duty-to-disclose instruction error was treated as harmless given a valid causing-to-be-done theory.
- The court rejected that a later agreement could retroactively cleanse unauthorized access.
- Supreme Court No. 25-1082: petition filed March 2026, denied 29 June 2026.
- A denial of review is not a new merits opinion endorsing the lower court's reasoning.
Caption: Evidence grade: Confirmed; do not read the appeal as still pending.
Notes: Use the amended analysis, not superseded wording. Sources [7][8].

## PART 2 | The $148M state deal is its own distinct outcome

California's 26 September 2018 announcement described a $148 million nationwide settlement over reporting and reasonable security, with all fifty states and DC as beneficiaries.

- California's announced portion was $26 million; a final judgment is linked for review.
- The national amount is a different actor and mechanism from the FTC and criminal matters.
- The settlement does not establish that the figure equals all harm caused.
- This study summarizes the AG announcement, not an exhaustive multistate analysis.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Intake route must not decide the classification

The following is original defensive analysis, not a finding about Uber's current organization. A report arriving through a bounty channel can still describe a completed intrusion.

- Separate three questions: weakness demonstrated, actions the party took, data now at risk.
- Define escalation triggers before a hard report arrives: sensitive-record acquisition, scope doubt, secrecy demands.
- A reward decision must name what conduct is rewarded and the evidence of eligibility.
- Preserve classification revisions: show what changed and which prior decisions to revisit.
Caption: Keep a reward decision from silently becoming "no incident occurred."
Notes: Original analysis grounded in the credential-to-cloud path. Sources [1][2].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Independent escalation keeps bad news accurate

Original analysis. Confidentiality is often needed, but material facts must stay accurate across security, legal, executive and oversight views. Assign a reviewer free to test the account.

- Response leaders, counsel and product teams carry incentives that make independent challenge valuable.
- Give staff a route to flag an omission without having to allege a legal violation.
- Record material disagreements and their resolution in a decision log.
- Separate direct observation, corroborated statements and unverified deletion assurances.
Caption: Do not let a payment milestone automatically close the exposure assessment.
Notes: Original analysis on escalation and deletion promises.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | What the record overstates and understates

The same facts can be stretched or shrunk. Faithful summaries resist both, keeping actors, mechanisms and legal weight distinct.

Left: Risks of overstating
- Calling it a bounty does not prove the conduct was in authorized scope.
- A deletion promise does not prove copies are gone.
- A single cost total hides distinct actors and purposes.
Right: Risks of understating
- Probation and a $50,000 fine are real outcomes, not an acquittal.
- The corporate NPA's accepted responsibility still stands.
- A cert denial is not a merits ruling to lean on.
Caption: Accuracy means refusing both the reassuring and the dismissive shortcut.
Notes: Draws on the executive assessment and the legal-outcome sections. Sources [1][2][6][8][9].

## CLOSE | Response ends with truthful decisions, not access

Uber 2016 connects a technical breach, corporate settlements, a non-prosecution agreement and an individual conviction, but these are not interchangeable.

- Credentials need limited authority; assurance claims need evidence; bounty programs need boundaries.
- The amended opinion and cert denial keep the account from stopping at an outdated stage.
- Above all, the decision process must preserve facts that challenge its preferred explanation.
- A response is incomplete if it addresses only access and not the truthfulness of later decisions.
Caption: Make accurate escalation durable.
Notes: The original defensive lesson over the verified record. Sources [1][7][8].
