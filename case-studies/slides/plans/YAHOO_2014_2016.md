## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY | Yahoo is account trust, not a single password list

Protecting passwords matters, but it does not answer whether an attacker can gain access through a trusted administrative or session mechanism.

- Mass account data, admin capability and session trust each confer authority a password control cannot address.
- Targeted intelligence collection ran alongside bulk theft, so account metadata is operationally consequential.
- The failure was partly organizational: technical knowledge did not become a corporate decision.
- The study treats 2014 as the main case and declines to collapse the layers into one headline.
Caption: Evidence grade: company disclosure and settled regulatory findings, read at source dates.
Notes: Draws on the 2016 annual report [1] and the SEC 2018 order [2]; see the Executive assessment.

## PART 1 | The 2014 intrusion surfaced only in 2016

Disclosure in 2016 does not mean the intrusion occurred in 2016.

- The annual report disclosed the 2014 incident on 22 September 2016, covering about 500 million accounts.
- Reported data: names, contact info, birthdates, bcrypt-hashed passwords, some security questions and answers.
- The independent committee found failures in inquiry, communication and management.
- It did not conclude that information was intentionally suppressed; the SEC later found misleading statements.
Caption: Evidence grade: company disclosure [1]; settled SEC findings [2].
Notes: Annual-report dates and data categories [1]; the SEC order on misleading disclosure and omissions [2].

## PART 1 | The 2013 breach stays a separate measurement

The chronology must keep the two populations distinct.

- A 3 October 2017 filing revised the 2013 theft scope to all roughly three billion accounts then existing.
- It recalled the 14 December 2016 estimate that more than one billion were likely affected.
- The revision was new intelligence on an old theft, not a new October 2017 breach.
- Do not add the 2013 and 2014 populations to claim three-and-a-half billion distinct people.
Caption: Evidence grade: a Confirmed company announcement filed with the SEC [3].
Notes: Revised scope and the prior estimate come from the 2017 announcement [3].

## PART 1 | Bulk data enabled selection of specific accounts

A large collection of metadata can drive targeting even when the intruder does not open every mailbox.

- DOJ charged Dokuchaev, Sushchin, Belan and Baratov, including two Russian FSB officers (15 March 2017).
- A copied user database and an account-management tool supported locating targets and forging cookies.
- Alleged unauthorized access reached at least 6,500 accounts through that mechanism.
- Recovery addresses and account relationships can reveal routes to other services.
Caption: Evidence grade: allegations when announced, not convictions [4].
Notes: The indictment summary, participant roles and the targeted-access figure, distinct from the database population [4].

## PART 1 | One guilty plea is the only verified outcome

The sentence is a concrete outcome for one defendant, not a conviction of the others.

- Baratov pleaded guilty in November 2017 and received a five-year sentence in May 2018.
- His role: hacking selected webmail accounts for a coconspirator and providing passwords for payment.
- The plea covered at least 80 accounts for an FSB coconspirator, plus more than 11,000 over several years.
- Those broader numbers are not accounts personally stolen from Yahoo's 2014 database.
- A later page-update date should not be confused with the date the sentence was imposed.
Caption: Evidence grade: admitted conduct within the plea's scope [5].
Notes: Plea, conduct scope and the five-year sentence from the May 2018 DOJ announcement [5].

## DEFENDER VIEW | Trust lives in data, support tools and sessions

Left: Identity data and support tools
- Treat recovery routes and account links as security-relevant, not just admin data.
- Inventory high-impact account actions and the identities authorized to perform them.
- Evaluate support tools by what they can cause, not whether the interface is labeled internal.
Right: Sessions
- Sessions are part of the credential system, not a separate concern.
- Test whether a password change or recovery actually revokes active sessions.
- Verify revocation across every authorized client and service path.
Caption: Original analysis: identity data, support authority and session trust.
Notes: Author's original analysis; the study cites no sources for these recommendations.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Response must cover every trust path, including links

A response built on one familiar path can miss another.

- Distinguish passwords, recovery mechanisms, active sessions, delegated apps and internal functions.
- Build a matrix linking each evidence category to the trust mechanism it affects.
- Sequence recovery changes so a fix does not lock out users or reopen the weakness.
- Metadata can connect services: assess plausible downstream risk without claiming all are breached.
- Separate direct findings from precautionary guidance in any user communication.
Caption: Original analysis: multi-path response and cross-service metadata risk.
Notes: Author's original analysis; no sources are cited for these controls.

## DEFENDER VIEW | Make knowledge travel and keep disclosure live

An incident team can know a serious fact while the organization acts on an outdated assumption.

- Define a process to escalate, acknowledge and record consequential technical facts.
- A message sent to a distribution list is not proof its implications were understood.
- Keep a register of public assertions with owners and defined review triggers.
- Preserve version history; do not quietly rewrite a prior statement.
- Give acquisition diligence a traceable incident trail, not a green dashboard.
Caption: Original analysis: escalation, living disclosure review and diligence.
Notes: Author's original analysis; no sources are cited for these practices.

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES | Headlines overstate counts; trust mechanisms stay understated

Left: Overstated in retellings
- Adding the 2013 and 2014 counts into one giant victim total.
- Reading 500M or 3B as unique people or confirmed victims.
- Treating the negotiated price cut as the audited cost of the breach.
Right: Understated in retellings
- Session and support-tool trust as access paths beyond the password.
- Metadata as a targeting asset, not harmless customer-administration data.
- The organizational gap between technical knowledge and business decisions.

## CLOSE | Be more precise, not merely more alarmed

A detailed case study should make the reader more precise.

- Keep the layers visible: mass data, selected access, session and admin trust, disclosure, outcomes.
- The separate 2013 breach is essential context, not a reason to merge numbers or attributions.
- Defenders: map every mechanism that can confer authority over an account.
- Researchers: retain dates, units and procedural status for every claim.
