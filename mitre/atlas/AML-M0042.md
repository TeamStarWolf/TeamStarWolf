# AML.M0042: Threat Intelligence Program

<a id="amlm0042"></a>

ATLAS: [AML.M0042](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0042), addresses 22 techniques

A Threat Intelligence Program enables organizations to proactively collect, analyze, and share information on threats to AI and cyber systems by leveraging internal observations and external reporting. The program supports decision-making processes, prioritizes defenses, and improves incident response by delivering actionable intelligence tailored to the organization's risk profile and operational environment. This mitigation can be implemented through the following measures: Establish an AI Threat Intelligence Team and Define and Requirements - Assign personnel to collect, analyze, and act on intelligence related to threats targeting AI systems. - Coordinate across cybersecurity, AI/ML development, and model evaluation teams to translate intelligence into actionable defenses. - Define intelligence requirements around critical AI assets, dependencies, deployment environments, and the decisions intelligence must support. - Prioritize collection according to relevant adversary objectives, capabilities, access requirements, and potential operational impacts. Include threats to AI components and the software, services, and infrastructure supporting them. Collect and Automate AI Threat Intelligence - Collect intelligence from internal sources, including AI application logs, model monitoring, red-team exercises, security incidents, and adversarial testing. - Monitor external sources, including AI vulnerability disclosures, incident reports, security research, and threat intelligence feeds. - Track active campaigns and adversary capabilities, including autonomous agent campaigns, reported use of zero-day vulnerabilities, and impersonation campaigns. - Collect information on compromised supply chains and published poisoned datasets, models, and AI agent tools. - Use threat intelligence platforms (TIPs) to aggregate, enrich, and disseminate AI-related threat intelligence. - Integrate intelligence with SIEMs and AI monitoring tools to correlate indicators of compromise with internal events. Analyze and Operationalize Intelligence - Map emerging and observed threats to MITRE ATLAS tactics and techniques to understand adversary behaviors and potential impacts. - Assess applicability to deployed components, versions, configurations, and workflows, including the access and conditions required for an attack. - Prioritize defensive actions based on threat relevance, AI system criticality, and potential operational impact. - Use intelligence to identify affected systems and artifacts, prioritize remediation or compensating controls, and update threat models, adversarial evaluations, detection logic, and incident response procedures. - Correlate relevant indicators and behaviors with internal telemetry to identify related activity. Validate detection logic against the organization's environment and available observations. - Assign defensive actions to responsible owners, track their completion, and verify whether they address the identified exposure. Share Intelligence and Continuously Improve - Share AI threat intelligence, attack patterns, and lessons learned with trusted industry partners and AI security communities. - Regularly update intelligence priorities as AI capabilities, vulnerabilities, and adversarial techniques evolve. - Evaluate program effectiveness based on improvements in threat detection, defensive coverage, and incident re sponse. Tools and Resources for Implementation - Threat Knowledge Bases and Intelligence Sources - MITRE ATLAS: Organize intelligence around documented adversary behaviors. - Vendor advisories, CVE/NVD, and CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog: Identify vulnerabilities affecting deployed components. - Supplier and repository security reporting: Identify compromised models, datasets, packages, containers, and AI agent tools. - Incident reports and security research: Assess targeting and attack methods, distinguishing operational activity from research demonstrations. - Intelligence Management and Sharing - Threat intelligence platforms, such as OpenCTI and MISP: Organize and share indicators, relationships, sources, and confidence assessments. - STIX and TAXII: Support structured intelligence exchange between compatible systems. - Case-management systems: Track investigations, assigned defensive actions, and outcomes. - Operational Analysis and Validation - SIEMs and AI monitoring tools: Correlate intelligence with internal telemetry to investigate related activity. - Asset and dependency inventories: Identify exposure to reported vulnerabilities and compromised artifacts. - Security evaluation capabilities: Turn intelligence into system-specific tests using appropriate manual and automated methods. - Community and Collaboration - ISACs and AI security communities: Exchange relevant campaign observations and defensive findings. - Supplier and platform reporting channels: Report malicious artifacts and compromised services to support investigation and removal.
