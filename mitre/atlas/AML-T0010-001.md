# AML.T0010.001: Software

<a id="amlt0010001"></a>

ATLAS tactics: initial-access  
Sub-technique of: [AML.T0010: Supply Chain Compromise](/mitre/atlas/AML-T0010.md)  
ATLAS: [AML.T0010.001](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0010.001)  

Adversaries may target software packages that are commonly used in AI-enabled systems or are part of the AI DevOps lifecycle. This can include deep learning frameworks used to build AI models (e.g. PyTorch, TensorFlow, Jax), generative AI integration frameworks (e.g. LangChain, LangFlow), inference engines, and AI DevOps tools. They may also target the dependency chains of any of these software packages. Additionally, adversaries may target specific components used by AI software such as configuration files or example usage of AI packages, which may be distributed in Jupyter notebooks. Adversaries may compromise legitimate packages or publish malicious software to a namesquatted location. They may target package names that are hallucinated by large language models (see: Publish Hallucinated Entities). They may also perform an AI Supply Chain Rug Pull in which they first publish a legitimate package and then publish a malicious version once they reach a critical mass of users.

## Mitigations (5)

- [AML.M0013: Code Signing](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0013.md): Enforce binary and application integrity with digital signature verification to prevent untrusted code from executing.
- [AML.M0014: Verify AI Artifacts](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0014.md): Verify the integrity and authenticity of AI artifacts before importing, loading, or deploying them.
- [AML.M0023: Maintain an AI Bill of Materials](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0023.md): Create and maintain an AI Bill of Materials (AI BOM) that identifies the components and dependencies used to build, evaluate, and operate an AI-enabled system.
- [AML.M0035: AI Red Team](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0035.md): Establish an AI red team responsible for conducting recurring, authorized, and threat-informed red-teaming exercises to identify and remediate vulnerabilities in AI-enabled systems before deployment and throughout operation.
- [AML.M0042: Threat Intelligence Program](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0042.md): A Threat Intelligence Program enables organizations to proactively collect, analyze, and share information on threats to AI and cyber systems by leveraging internal observations and external reporting.
