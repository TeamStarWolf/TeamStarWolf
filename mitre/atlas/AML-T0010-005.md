# AML.T0010.005: AI Agent Tool

<a id="amlt0010005"></a>

ATLAS tactics: initial-access  
Sub-technique of: [AML.T0010: Supply Chain Compromise](/mitre/atlas/AML-T0010.md)  
ATLAS: [AML.T0010.005](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0010.005)  

Adversaries may target AI agent tools as a means to compromise a victim's AI supply chain. Tools add capabilities to AI agents, allowing them to interact with other services, connect to data sources, access internet resources, run system tools, and execute code. They are an attractive target for adversaries because compromising an AI agent can provide them with broad access and permissions on the victim's system via the agent's other tools. Poisoned AI agent tools may contain malicious definitions or instructions, hidden executable logic, or deliberately poisoned runtime responses (See AI Agent Tool Poisoning). These changes may manipulate the agent's behavior, modify how other tools are invoked, introduce hidden side effects, or provide unauthorized access to data and services. Adversaries have successfully used a poisoned MCP server to exfiltrate private user data. Agent tools have exploded in popularity, with thousands of MCP servers available publicly. They are often released on open-source software repositories such as GitHub, indexed on hubs specific to MCP servers, and published to package registries such as NPM. AI agents can also be connected to remotely-hosted tools. This creates an environment where malicious tools can proliferate rapidly and safeguards are often not in place.

## Mitigations (3)

- [AML.M0023: Maintain an AI Bill of Materials](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0023.md): Create and maintain an AI Bill of Materials (AI BOM) that identifies the components and dependencies used to build, evaluate, and operate an AI-enabled system.
- [AML.M0035: AI Red Team](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0035.md): Establish an AI red team responsible for conducting recurring, authorized, and threat-informed red-teaming exercises to identify and remediate vulnerabilities in AI-enabled systems before deployment and throughout operation.
- [AML.M0042: Threat Intelligence Program](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0042.md): A Threat Intelligence Program enables organizations to proactively collect, analyze, and share information on threats to AI and cyber systems by leveraging internal observations and external reporting.
