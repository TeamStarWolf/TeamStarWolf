# AML.T0010: Supply Chain Compromise

<a id="amlt0010"></a>

ATLAS tactics: initial-access  
ATLAS: [AML.T0010](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0010)  

Adversaries may manipulate products, components, or their development and delivery mechanisms before they reach downstream consumers to compromise data or systems. Manipulation may involve source code, dependencies, development tools, build environments, software updates, distribution channels, or hardware. In AI environments, targets may include Hardware, Software, Data Repositories, Model Repositories, Container Registries, and AI Agent Tools. Compromised components may introduce malicious executable behavior, alter model behavior, or provide unauthorized access when incorporated into downstream systems. Autonomous AI agents may also select and conduct supply-chain attacks against software or services outside the AI supply chain. An agent may develop a malicious contribution, submit it through an established development workflow, and attempt to persuade maintainers to approve and distribute it.

## Mitigations (4)

- [AML.M0014: Verify AI Artifacts](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0014.md): Verify the integrity and authenticity of AI artifacts before importing, loading, or deploying them.
- [AML.M0023: Maintain an AI Bill of Materials](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0023.md): Create and maintain an AI Bill of Materials (AI BOM) that identifies the components and dependencies used to build, evaluate, and operate an AI-enabled system.
- [AML.M0035: AI Red Team](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0035.md): Establish an AI red team responsible for conducting recurring, authorized, and threat-informed red-teaming exercises to identify and remediate vulnerabilities in AI-enabled systems before deployment and throughout operation.
- [AML.M0042: Threat Intelligence Program](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0042.md): A Threat Intelligence Program enables organizations to proactively collect, analyze, and share information on threats to AI and cyber systems by leveraging internal observations and external reporting.
