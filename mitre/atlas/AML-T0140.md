# AML.T0140: Centralized AI Service Request Routing

<a id="amlt0140"></a>

ATLAS tactics: defense-evasion  
ATLAS: [AML.T0140](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0140)  

Adversaries may distribute coordinated AI service requests across multiple pathways to impede detection, conceal the coordinated nature of the requests, and evade AI provider enforcement. Distributing activity can limit the portion of an operation visible through any individual account or pathway, obscuring its aggregate scale and common objective. Adversaries may use centralized routing infrastructure to divide a workload or prompt suite across multiple pathways, including various service APIs, cloud-hosted endpoints, third-party aggregators or relays, and account pools. Routers may rotate identities and request pathways, mix adversarial requests with unrelated customer traffic, and automatically redirect requests when requests are blocked. These capabilities allow adversaries to maintain coordinated operations while making the activity appear to originate from unrelated users or services. Adversaries may also use routing infrastructure to centrally optimize API costs and sustain distributed operations within available quotas and budgets. This may include prioritizing cost-efficient access pathways, allocating quotas across accounts to align with operational budgets, segmenting accounts by purpose, and organizing requests to maximize cache reuse. Centralized routing may coordinate shifts across accounts, providers, or pathways in response to pricing or rate-limit changes, enabling an operation to adapt while continuing to obscure its common control and aggregate activity. This behavior may be implemented via proxy services (See Acquire Infrastructure: AI Service Proxies), multiple cloud instances, IP pools, coordination across multiple accounts, or other distributed infrastructure.

## Mitigations (1)

- [AML.M0042: Threat Intelligence Program](/mitre/atlas/AML-M0042.md): A Threat Intelligence Program enables organizations to proactively collect, analyze, and share information on threats to AI and cyber systems by leveraging internal observations and external reporting.
