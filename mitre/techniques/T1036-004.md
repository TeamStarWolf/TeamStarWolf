# T1036.004 — Masquerade Task or Service

<a id="t1036004"></a>

**Tactics:** defense-evasion  
**Platforms:** Linux, macOS, Windows  
**ATT&CK:** [T1036.004](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1036/004)  
**Sub-technique of:** [T1036 — Masquerading](/mitre/techniques/T1036.md)  

Adversaries may attempt to manipulate the name of a task or service to make it appear legitimate or benign. Tasks/services executed by the Task Scheduler or systemd will typically be given a name and/or description. Windows services will have a service name as well as a display name. Many benign tasks and services exist that have commonly associated names. Adversaries may give tasks or services names that are similar or identical to those of legitimate ones. Tasks or services contain other fields, such as a description, that adversaries may attempt to make appear legitimate.

## D3FEND countermeasures (1)

- [Scheduled Job Analysis](/mitre/d3fend/scheduled-job-analysis.md) (Detect) — analyzes. Analysis of source files, processes, destination files, or destination servers associated with a scheduled job to detect unauthorized use of job scheduling.

## Detection

**Detection strategies:**

- Detection of Masqueraded Tasks or Services with Suspicious Naming and Execution

**Analytics (3):**

- **Analytic 0324** (Windows) — `WinEventLog:System EventCode=7045` → Service Creation; `WinEventLog:Security EventCode=4698` → Scheduled Job Creation; `WinEventLog:Sysmon EventCode=1` → Process Creation
- **Analytic 0325** (Linux) — `auditd:CONFIG_CHANGE /var/log/audit/audit.log` → Scheduled Job Modification; `linux:osquery scheduled/real-time` → Service Metadata; `linux:cron cron activity` → Scheduled Job Metadata
- **Analytic 0326** (macOS) — `fs:fileevents /Library/LaunchDaemons/*.plist, ~/Library/LaunchAgents/*.plist` → Scheduled Job Metadata; `macos:endpointsecurity ES_EVENT_TYPE_NOTIFY_EXEC` → Process Creation; `macos:unifiedlog subsystem=com.apple.launchservices` → Service Metadata
