# T1656 — Impersonation

<a id="t1656"></a>

**Tactics:** defense-evasion  
**Platforms:** Linux, macOS, Office Suite, SaaS, Windows  
**ATT&CK:** [T1656](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1656)  

> **⚠ Revoked in ATT&CK v19.2** — superseded by [T1684.001 — Impersonation](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1684/001).

Adversaries may impersonate a trusted person or organization in order to persuade and trick a target into performing some action on their behalf. For example, adversaries may communicate with victims (via [Phishing for Information](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1598), [Phishing](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1566), or [Internal Spearphishing](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1534)) while impersonating a known sender such as an executive, colleague, or third-party vendor. Established trust can then be leveraged to accomplish an adversary’s ultimate goals, possibly against multiple victims. In many cases of business email compromise or email fraud campaigns, adversaries use impersonation to defraud victims -- deceiving them into sending money or divulging information that ultimately enables [Financial Theft](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1657). Adversaries will often also use social engineering techniques such as manipulative and persuasive language in email subject lines and body text such as `payment`, `request`, or `urgent` to push the victim to act quickly before malicious activity is detected. These campaigns are often specifically targeted against people who, due to job roles and/or accesses, can carry out the adversary’s goal. Impersonation is typically preceded by reconnaissance techniques such as [Gather Victim Identity Information](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1589) and [Gather Victim Org Information](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1591) as well as acquiring infrastructure such as email domains (i.e. [Domains](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1583/001)) to substantiate their false identity. There is the potential for multiple victims in campaigns involving impersonation. For example, an adversary may [Compromise Accounts](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1586) targeting one organization which can then be used to support impersonation against other entities.

**Analytics (5):**

- **Analytic 0792** (Windows) — `WinEventLog:Security EventCode=4624, 4648` → Logon Session Creation; `m365:unified SendOnBehalf/SendAs: Emails sent where the sending identity mismatches account ownership` → Application Log Content
- **Analytic 0793** (Linux) — `auditd:SYSCALL execve: Processes executing sendmail/postfix with forged headers` → Command Execution; `Application:Mail Mismatch between authenticated username and From header in email` → Application Log Content
- **Analytic 0794** (macOS) — `macos:unifiedlog Mail.app or third-party clients sending messages with mismatched From headers` → Application Log Content
- **Analytic 0795** (SaaS) — `gcp:workspaceaudit SendAs: Outbound messages with alias identities that differ from primary account` → Application Log Content
- **Analytic 0796** (Office Suite) — `m365:unified SendOnBehalf/SendAs: Office Suite initiated messages using impersonated identities` → Application Log Content

_Notable groups seen using this technique:_

- [Kimsuky](/mitre/groups/G0094.md) (aka Black Banshee, Velvet Chollima, Emerald Sleet, THALLIUM) — Kimsuky is a North Korea-based cyber espionage group that has been active since at least 2012. The group initially targeted South Korean government agencies, think tanks, and subject-matter experts in various fields.
- [APT41](/mitre/groups/G0096.md) (aka Wicked Panda, Brass Typhoon, BARIUM) — APT41 is a threat group that researchers have assessed as Chinese state-sponsored espionage group that also conducts financially-motivated operations.
- [LAPSUS$](/mitre/groups/G1004.md) (aka DEV-0537, Strawberry Tempest) — LAPSUS$ is cyber criminal threat group that has been active since at least mid-2021.
- [Scattered Spider](/mitre/groups/G1015.md) (aka Roasted 0ktapus, Octo Tempest, Storm-0875, UNC3944) — Scattered Spider is a native English-speaking cybercriminal group active since at least 2022. The group initially targeted customer relationship management (CRM) providers, business process outsourcing (BPO) firms, and…
- [Saint Bear](/mitre/groups/G1031.md) (aka Storm-0587, TA471, UAC-0056, Lorec53) — Saint Bear is a Russian-nexus threat actor active since early 2021, primarily targeting entities in Ukraine and Georgia.
- [APT42](/mitre/groups/G1044.md) — APT42 is an Iranian-sponsored threat group that conducts cyber espionage and surveillance.
