# T1696.001: Automate CAPTCHA Completion

<a id="t1696001"></a>

Tactics: stealth  
Platforms: SaaS  
ATT&CK: [T1696.001](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1696/001)  
Sub-technique of: [T1696: Anti-Automation Evasion](/mitre/techniques/T1696.md)  

> New in ATT&CK v20.0. MITRE added this technique in the current release. The overview, defender notes, references and related ATLAS techniques on this page are the library's own, last reviewed 2026-11-03; the NIST control and corpus signal data do not cover it yet.

Solving a CAPTCHA no longer takes a person at the keyboard. Solver services, browser-automation frameworks and image or audio recognition models return a valid answer in seconds, so one operator can create accounts or submit forms at the scale the challenge was meant to stop.

Unit 42's 2023 report on PurpleUrchin, run by the group it calls Automated Libra, describes a freejacking operation that bypassed CAPTCHA while signing up for limited-time cloud trials to mine cryptocurrency.

ATT&CK's definition: Adversaries may attempt to use bots to successfully solve CAPTCHA challenges. CAPTCHA challenges are typically used by defenders to detect and prevent bot and spam traffic from accessing protected webpages.

## Detection

Detection strategies:

- Detection of Automated CAPTCHA

Analytics (1):

- Analytic 2104 (SaaS): Successful CAPTCHA completion does not expose whether the challenge was solved manually, through automated tooling, through an AI-enabled solver, or through a third-party human-solving service. Providers may independently classify associated sessions as automated or bot-like, but this identifies suspicious automation rather than the method used to complete the CAPTCHA.

Defender notes (library): A solved challenge looks the same whether a person, a script or a paid solving service answered it, which is why MITRE's analytic stops at the provider's own bot score. Watch the steps around the challenge instead: challenge tokens redeemed from a different IP or client than the one that requested them, solve times that are unusually uniform, many new accounts with the same payment, email-domain or naming pattern, and trial or free-tier resources that start heavy compute (for example cryptocurrency mining) soon after sign-up.
