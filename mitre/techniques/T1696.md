# T1696: Anti-Automation Evasion

<a id="t1696"></a>

Tactics: stealth  
Platforms: SaaS  
ATT&CK: [T1696](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1696)  

Live exposure: [Open T1696 in ATTACK-Navi](https://teamstarwolf.github.io/ATTACK-Navi/#/matrix?tech=T1696) for its CVEs (CTID CVE-to-ATT&CK mappings), KEV/EPSS priority, detection analytics and candidate defenses.

> New in ATT&CK v20.0. MITRE added this technique in the current release. The overview, defender notes, references and related ATLAS techniques on this page are the library's own, last reviewed 2026-11-03; the NIST control and corpus signal data do not cover it yet.

Anyone who runs a sign-up form, a login page or a ticket queue has met this problem: the CAPTCHA or bot check meant to keep scripts out is now something an attacker can buy as a service or hand to an AI browser agent. This technique covers getting automated traffic past those checks, either by completing the challenge with a solver (T1696.001) or by behaving enough like a person that no challenge is shown (T1696.002).

The practical lesson for defenders is that a passed check is weak evidence that a person is present. Bot checks still raise the cost of abuse, but they work best as one signal among several rather than as the gate itself.

ATT&CK's definition: Adversaries may employ various means to evade or complete automation checks. Automation checks distinguish human users from bot traffic and prevent bots from reaching the web pages protected by automation checks.

## Detection

Detection strategies:

- Detection of Anti-Automation Evasion

Analytics (1):

- Analytic 2106 (SaaS): No target-side behavioral Detection Strategy exists for CAPTCHA based solely on CAPTCHA telemetry.

Defender notes (library): MITRE's analytic for this technique says CAPTCHA telemetry alone cannot separate a person from a solver, so look at what the session does after the check. Useful signals are sign-up or login bursts that share an IP range, ASN, residential proxy pool or browser fingerprint; accounts that pass the check and then move straight to the abuse the check protects (bulk messages, trial resource creation, form spam); and challenge pass rates or solve times that shift sharply for one form or one client population. Bot-management and WAF logs, account-creation logs and the protected application's own audit log are the sources to join.
