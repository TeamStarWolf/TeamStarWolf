# T1697: Steal Application Specific Passwords

<a id="t1697"></a>

Tactics: credential-access  
Platforms: Office Suite, Identity Provider, SaaS  
ATT&CK: [T1697](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1697)  

Live exposure: [Open T1697 in ATTACK-Navi](https://teamstarwolf.github.io/ATTACK-Navi/#/matrix?tech=T1697) for its CVEs (CTID CVE-to-ATT&CK mappings), KEV/EPSS priority, detection analytics and candidate defenses.

> New in ATT&CK v20.0. MITRE added this technique in the current release. The overview, defender notes, references and related ATLAS techniques on this page are the library's own, last reviewed 2026-11-03; the NIST control and corpus signal data do not cover it yet.

Application-specific passwords exist so that an old mail client or device that cannot do multifactor prompts can still sign in. That makes them a quiet way around MFA: a password the user creates once, that the provider accepts without a second factor, and that keeps working until someone revokes it.

Citizen Lab and Google reported in June 2025 that an attacker posing as a U.S. State Department employee persuaded a researcher to create app-specific passwords and send them over, and Google attributed the activity to a Russian state-backed operator. ATT&CK lists APT42 as a procedure example.

ATT&CK's definition: Adversaries may steal Application Specific Passwords (ASPs) to gain access to Valid Accounts, bypassing multifactor authentication (MFA) methods. ASPs are passwords typically used to give third-party applications or legacy devices (which do not support MFA methods) account access.

## Mitigations (4)

- [M1017: User Training](/mitre/mitigations/M1017.md): User Training involves educating employees and contractors on recognizing, reporting, and preventing cyber threats that rely on human interaction, such as phishing, social engineering, and other manipulative techniques.
- [M1018: User Account Management](/mitre/mitigations/M1018.md): User Account Management involves implementing and enforcing policies for the lifecycle of user accounts, including creation, modification, and deactivation.
- [M1036: Account Use Policies](/mitre/mitigations/M1036.md): Account Use Policies help mitigate unauthorized access by configuring and enforcing rules that govern how and when accounts can be used.
- [M1042: Disable or Remove Feature or Program](/mitre/mitigations/M1042.md): Disable or remove unnecessary and potentially vulnerable software, features, or services to reduce the attack surface and prevent abuse by adversaries.

## Detection

Detection strategies:

- Suspicious Acquisition and Use of Application-Specific Passwords

Analytics (3):

- Analytic 2075 (Identity Provider): `idp:audit Application-specific password created/added for account ` -> User Account Modification; `idp:authentication  Successful authentication associated with legacy/application-specific credential flow ` -> User Account Authentication; `idp:session  New session established from source/device inconsistent with account baseline ` -> Logon Session Creation
- Analytic 2076 (Office Suite): `m365:unified Authentication/account audit event identifying app-password or applicable legacy-authentication activity ` -> Application Log Content; `azure:signinlogs Successful account authentication with authentication/client properties inconsistent with normal MFA-backed access ` -> User Account Authentication; `idp:audit ASP added/created where exposed by provider` -> User Account Modification
- Analytic 2077 (SaaS): `saas:audit  Successful application-specific/legacy credential authentication where exposed ` -> User Account Authentication; `saas:audit ASP creation/modification event where supported ` -> User Account Modification; `saas:session  New authenticated session associated with changed source/client context ` -> Logon Session Creation

Defender notes (library): MITRE ships analytics for identity-provider, Office Suite and SaaS logs. In practice, alert on every app-specific password creation for accounts that have no approved need for one, and correlate it with sign-ins through legacy or non-MFA protocols (IMAP, POP, SMTP AUTH and similar) from a new source within a short window. Where the provider allows it, turn app-specific passwords off for most users and review the ones that remain; a request from "IT" or an outside contact to create one and share it is a phishing signal on its own.

## Adversary usage

Threat groups (1): [APT42](/mitre/groups/G1044.md)

_Notable groups seen using this technique:_

- [APT42](/mitre/groups/G1044.md): [APT42](https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G1044) is an Iranian-sponsored threat group that conducts cyber espionage and surveillance.(Citation: Mandiant APT42-charms) The group primarily focuses on targets in the Middle East region, but has targeted a variety of industries and countries since at least 2015.(Citation: Mandiant APT42-charms) [APT42](https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G1044) starts cyber operations through spearphishing emails and/or the PINEFLOWER Android malware, then monitors and collects information from the compromised systems and devices.(Citation: Mandiant APT42-charms) Finally, [APT42](https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G1044) exfiltrates data using native features and open-source tools.(Citation: Mandiant APT42-untangling) [APT42](https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G1044) activities have been linked to [Magic Hound](https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G0059) by other commercial vendors.
