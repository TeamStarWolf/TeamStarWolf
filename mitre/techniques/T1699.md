# T1699: Direct Prompt Injection

<a id="t1699"></a>

Tactics: execution  
Platforms: Windows, SaaS  
ATT&CK: [T1699](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1699)  

> New in ATT&CK v20.0. MITRE added this technique in the current release. The overview, defender notes, references and related ATLAS techniques on this page are the library's own, last reviewed 2026-11-03; the NIST control and corpus signal data do not cover it yet.

Direct prompt injection is the attacker typing into the model themselves: role-play framings, "ignore your previous instructions", requests split into harmless-looking pieces, or claims to be a security tester, all aimed at getting the model to do what its safeguards or its operator would refuse.

ATT&CK lists two campaigns as procedure examples: the espionage campaign Anthropic described in November 2025 as the first reported AI-orchestrated one, and an AI-assisted breach of Mexican government infrastructure described in an April 2026 report. In both, prompting a model past its safeguards was part of how the operators worked.

ATT&CK's definition: Adversaries may input malicious prompts directly into an LLM’s user interface. The prompts can be designed to bypass safeguards, manipulate context, override previous instructions, or coerce the LLM into unauthorized behavior.

## Detection

MITRE publishes no detection strategy for this technique in ATT&CK v20.0.

Defender notes (library): For assistants your organization runs, log prompts and responses at an AI gateway and review accounts that show repeated refusals followed by success, override phrasing ("ignore", "developer mode", "system prompt"), attempts to read the system prompt, or claims of authorization (pentest, red team) without a matching record. For agents with tools, the higher-value signal is what the model does after the prompt: tool calls, code execution or data access that the user's role would not normally produce.
